# Chapter 4: Physical Education & Sports for CWSN (Children with Special Needs)

## Adaptive Physical Education

A diversified program of developmental activities, games and sports suited to the interests, capacities and limitations of students with disabilities.

**Aim:** To ensure physical education for children with disabilities is delivered by a qualified Adapted Physical Educator.

**Needs for Adaptive PE:**

1. Children with disabilities have special needs because they can't take part in regular physical exercises/activities
2. Adaptive PE offers developmental activities/games/sports suited to their interests, capacities and limitations
3. Focuses on individualised programs for safe/successful participation in PE
4. Provides safe, personally satisfying, successful experiences of physical activity for differently-abled children
5. Focuses on development of composure, posture, balance, and coordinative motor skills

**Aims of Adaptive PE:**

1. Providing PE that meets the unique needs of children
2. Development of students' motor skills
3. Assisting differently-abled children in achieving mental, emotional, social & physical growth
4. Achieving meaningful success and positive educational outcomes
5. Developing self-esteem and improving socialization skills
6. Coping with disabilities through an active, healthy lifestyle
7. Developing cognitive abilities to improve educational results

**Objectives of Adaptive PE:**

1. Develop motor skills
2. Improve physical fitness
3. Ensure students' participation in PE programs
4. Promote sportsmanship qualities
5. Ensure services to differently-abled students
6. Improve social adjustment
7. Develop knowledge of good mechanisms

## Disability

Defined as a condition or function judged to be significantly impaired relative to the usual standard for an individual — a restriction or lack of ability to perform an activity in the manner (or within the range) considered normal for a human being.

**1. Intellectual Disability**
Characterised by significant limitation in both intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour. Originates before age 18; marked by low IQ scope and significant problems adapting to new situations.

- **Symptoms:** Very little interpersonal skills; not following rules/instructions; reduced capacity to learn/solve problems/reason; lack of practical skills (e.g. activities of daily living)

**2. Physical Disability**
Long-term loss or impairment of a body part that limits physical function; includes motor deficiency, sensory impairment, visual/hearing impairment, and limitation to mobility/stamina.

- **Symptoms:** Lack of full range of mobility; problems related to senses (sight, hearing, speech impairment); lack of motor skill/missed developmental milestones; lack of control over limbs/body parts

**Causes and Nature of Disability:**
Accident • Poverty • War • Nuclear accidents • Poor access to healthcare • Inherited disabilities • Illness • Malnutrition • Poison & pesticides • Lack of education • Medicines & vaccines (adverse effects) • Dangerous working conditions

## Concept of Disorder

A disorder is an ailment that disturbs a person's health; it hinders performance and diminishes efficiency.

**Changes due to disorder:**

1. Changes behaviour, thought and emotion, possibly causing significant distress to self or others
2. Person is unable to meet their own needs, or is at risk
3. Starts small but, if untreated, can grow serious and progress into a disability

**Types of Disorder:** Mental disorder, Neurological disorder, Hyperactivity disorder, Eating disorder, Addiction disorder, Attention disorder

**Prevention:** Regular exercise; Physical education (increases mental control)

## Disability Etiquette

A set of guidelines on how to approach people with mental/physical disabilities — treating them with respect and care, and helping bring them into normal life. (The Disability Rights Movement, ~1970, is said to have brought world attention to disabled people.)

**General Guidelines:**

- Always respect the dignity of a disabled person
- When introduced, it's appropriate to shake hands
- Never patronise a wheelchair user by patting them on the head/shoulder
- Respect the individuality of the person
- Talk to the person directly
- If you offer assistance, wait until the person responds
- Don't show extra attention/added care — it can feel like sympathy
- Respect the person's privacy
- When someone with a disability asks for assistance, respond graciously

**Specific Guidelines:**

| Condition                         | Guidelines                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Speech difficulties**           | Give attention to the person; allow extra conversation time; use short, simple, concrete sentences                                                                                     |
| **Hearing loss**                  | Get attention with a wave/shoulder tap; speak clearly & slowly without exaggerating lip movement; use sign language if familiar to both                                                |
| **Vision loss**                   | Announce yourself when entering/leaving a room; communicate written info orally; offer your arm if walking together; not everyone reads Braille — ask for their preferred alternative |
| **Cognitive/language impairment** | Use simple, short sentences; treat with respect & dignity; give extra time to respond; encourage rather than correct                                                                   |
| **Mobility impairment**           | Don't push/pull/touch their wheelchair or walking aids; place items within easy reach; step down or bend to their level when speaking                                                  |

## Role of Professionals for Children with Special Needs (CWSN)

| Professional               | Role                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Counsellor**             | Trusted person providing a safe, supportive environment; discusses the child's concerns/feelings; helps with academic, social, personal & career development; runs programs on stress/anger/conflict management and positive thinking; helps the child cope better with the disability                                                 |
| **Occupational Therapist** | Helps CWSN children develop fine and gross motor skills (eating, dressing, bathing, drinking); develops sensory & cognitive development; maximises the child's potential to take part in everyday activities; develops physical coordination (e.g. hand–eye coordination)                                                             |
| **Physical Therapist**     | Manages children with movement disorders; teaches how to balance movement while walking, rolling, sitting, crawling; encourages independence/mobility, building self-esteem; evaluates movement & function with reference to mobility, balance, posture, fatigue; uses techniques like massage, exercise, electrotherapy, hydrotherapy |
| **PE Teacher**             | Determines the ability of CWSN students and the procedures needed to support participation in sports/fitness; teaches physical activities that reduce stress, depression and anxiety                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Speech Therapist**       | Builds communication skills — words, sounds of speech, gestures, eye contact; teaches children to express themselves via oral communication, aiding emotional development and confidence                                                                                                                                              |
| **Special Educator**       | Works for overall development of CWSN children; improves social, emotional & behavioural functioning; encourages family/school staff to understand the child's needs                                                                                                                                                                   |
