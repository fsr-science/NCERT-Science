## 1. Meaning and Importance of Yoga

### Meaning

- The word **Yoga** comes from the Sanskrit root **'yuj'**, meaning **to unite or to join**.
- Yoga is the oldest Indian way of dealing with the fitness (physical and mental) of the body, mind and spirit. Its roots are in India — sadhus (saints) practised it thousands of years ago.
- It is **not just a series of twisted body poses**. It helps the individual connect with their inner spirit, which is essentially divine and connected to the "universal spirit" or "God".
- Yoga is a **science** dealing with the health of the body and the harmony of the mind.
- **Ultimate aim:** self-identification and self-perfection, which come through **self-purification and self-realization**.
- *Class-notes definition:* Yoga implies the unification of the physical, mental, intellectual and spiritual aspects of a human being; it develops a person's consciousness.

### Definitions of Yoga

| Source | Definition |
| ------ | ---------- |
| **Bhagwad Gita** | *"Samatvam yoga uchyate"* — yoga is balance and harmony of mind and body. *"Yoga Karmasu Kaushalam"* — yoga is skill in work |
| **Shankracharya** | Yoga is withdrawal of the sense organs from worldly objects and their control through yoga |
| **Swami Digamber ji** | Yoga is a union of *Atma* (individual soul) and *Parmatma* (supreme soul) |
| **Maharishi Patanjali** | "Checking the impulses of the mind is Yoga" |
| **Dictionary** | A set of physical and mental exercises |

> 💡 **Yoga Sutra** — the authoritative text on yoga, written by sage **Patanjali**. It is a collection of aphorisms outlining the **eight limbs of yoga**, giving guidelines for a meaningful and purposeful life.

### Importance of Yoga

Yoga promotes overall well-being — physical health, mental clarity and emotional balance. It harmonises body, mind and spirit, and is useful for people of all ages and backgrounds.

| # | Importance | Explanation |
| - | ---------- | ----------- |
| 1 | **Reduces tension** | Regular practice relaxes the body; *Dharana* and *Dhyana* give peace of mind |
| 2 | **Improves concentration** | Techniques develop concentration → better memory and mental sharpness |
| 3 | **Cleans respiratory organs** | Neti, Dhauti, Kapalabhati etc. keep the respiratory organs clean |
| 4 | **Controls sense organs** | Develops the ability to make sense organs function properly as per need |
| 5 | **Relaxes the body** | Rest removes fatigue; *Shavasana* and *Makarasana* are very beneficial |
| 6 | **Shapes the body** | Keeps the body slim and controls body weight |
| 7 | **Cures various diseases** | Prevents as well as cures many diseases — asthma, high blood pressure, heart problems, arthritis, lower back pain, PCOS, etc. |
| 8 | **Reduces obesity** | Yogic exercises help; meditative asanas reduce mental tension, which also helps |
| 9 | **Improves health** | Strengthens muscles and regulates respiratory, circulatory, nervous and digestive systems |
| 10 | **Maintains correct posture** | Prevents postural deformities |
| 11 | **Increases flexibility** | Makes movement efficient and graceful; helps prevent sports injury (e.g. *Chakrasana, Dhanurasana, Halasana*) |
| 12 | **Physical purity & relaxation** | *(class notes)* Brings physical purity and gives relaxation |
| 13 | **Spiritual and moral development** | *(class notes)* Develops people spiritually; improves moral and ethical values |

### Overall Benefits of Yoga

- Improves flexibility, breathing and balance
- Lowers blood pressure and relieves stress
- Increases stamina, bone density and muscle strength
- Boosts mental and spiritual well-being

## 2. Ashtanga Yoga (8 Limbs)

**Ashtanga** = *Ashta* (eight) + *Anga* (limbs) → **eight-limbed yoga**, described by **Sage Patanjali** in the Yoga Sutras. The limbs are arranged in sequence from the **outer to the inner self**; the ultimate goal is the union of the soul with the divine.

| # | Limb | Meaning |
| - | ---- | ------- |
| 1 | **Yama** | Restraints / moral principles |
| 2 | **Niyama** | Observances (self-discipline) |
| 3 | **Asana** | Postures |
| 4 | **Pranayama** | Regulation of breath |
| 5 | **Pratyahara** | Withdrawal of the senses |
| 6 | **Dharana** | Concentration of the mind |
| 7 | **Dhyana** | Meditation |
| 8 | **Samadhi** | Ultimate union of the individual soul with God |

### I. Yama (Restraints)

Five principles forming a code of conduct for interacting with the world. Also called "the five restraints" because they describe what one should **avoid** to advance on the spiritual path.

| Yama | Meaning | Explanation |
| ---- | ------- | ----------- |
| **Ahimsa** | Non-violence | Shun all acts of violence — physical *and* mental. Resist jealousy, anger and hate for any being |
| **Satya** | Truthfulness | Be truthful in thought, word and deed. Hiding the truth from someone is also lying |
| **Asteya** | Non-stealing | Stealing is a crime legally and morally. Using someone's possessions or thoughts for one's own benefit is wrong |
| **Brahmacharya** | Wise use of energy | Keep the sense organs under control; direct energy away from external desires towards peace and happiness within *(class notes: celibacy)* |
| **Aparigraha** | Non-coveting / non-possessiveness | Live with the minimum things needed to sustain life; do not desire physical or material pleasures |

### II. Niyama (Observances)

Inward practices to improve the self — five essential teachings.

| Niyama | Meaning | Explanation |
| ------ | ------- | ----------- |
| **Saucha** | Purification | Cleanliness of body and mind — outer body free of dirt, inner organs functioning well, mind free of wrong thoughts |
| **Santosha** | Contentment | Be satisfied with what you have. More expectations = more frustration |
| **Tapas** | Asceticism / discipline | Keep the mind detached, controlled and tolerant of heat, cold, hunger etc. Focus all energy on life goals |
| **Swadhyaya** | Self-study | Introspect — who am I, what is my aim, how can I give back to society? |
| **Ishvara Pranidhana** | Dedication to God | *Ishvara* = Supreme Being; *Pranidhana* = fixing. Complete surrender of self to God in words, deeds and thoughts |

### III. Asana

Various postures of yoga. They keep a person healthy and balanced, develop concentration, and build agility, balance, endurance and vitality. They keep the body free from disease; their real importance lies in how they **train and discipline the mind**.

- **Types:** Corrective asanas • Relaxative asanas • Meditative asanas
- **Benefits:** Good for inner organs; reduces excess fat; regulates blood and oxygen flow; increases flexibility and agility by stretching muscles.

### IV. Pranayama

Fourth step of Ashtanga yoga — practice of breathing techniques. It improves the respiratory system, and regular practice helps control the mind and emotions. Three stages: **Puraka** (inhale), **Kumbhaka** (retain), **Rechaka** (exhale). *(Detailed in [Section 4](#4-pranayama-and-its-types).)*

### V. Pratyahara

Detaching the mind and sense organs from their related activity and attaching them to God. The senses no longer respond to external objects (words, beauty, taste, smell…) that hinder concentration and divert a person from self-welfare. The practitioner stops responding to external stimuli and starts meditating inward.

### VI. Dharana

Concentration of the mind — wholly on a single point or a task one is completely engrossed in. The first step towards *samadhi*; a mental exercise that enables a yogi to go ahead to *dhyana* and *samadhi*.

### VII. Dhyana

Meditation — the seventh limb. Complete concentration of the mind over a period of time **without any divergence**; the stage prior to samadhi. In this state one can integrate the body and control breath, senses, mind and ego.

### VIII. Samadhi

The stage where one **loses personal identity** — the peak of meditation and the union of the individual soul with the supreme soul. The senses are at rest as in sleep, but the mind remains alert and fully conscious.

> 💡 All eight limbs together improve physical health and mental, emotional, moral, social and spiritual balance — developing the personality and helping one live long and enjoy life.

## 3. Yogic Kriyas (Shat Karma)

**Background:** In Ayurveda, the *Tridosha* theory says the body is made of three elements — **Vata, Pitta and Kapha**. Imbalance causes disease. To restore balance, yoga recommends **six purification techniques — Shat Kriyas** (also *Shuddhikriya*).

Kriyas are cleansing techniques of **Hatha Yoga** that purify internal and external organs, remove toxins, improve organ function, and prepare the body for advanced yogic practice.

> ⚠️ Kriyas, especially Dhauti, Basti and Sutra Neti, must be learned **only under an expert yoga teacher**.

### Overview

| Kriya | Function | Types |
| ----- | -------- | ----- |
| **Neti** | Purifies nasal passages, clears respiratory blockages | Jalaneti, Sutra Neti |
| **Dhauti** | Cleanses stomach and digestive tract (oesophagus & stomach) to remove toxins | Vamana, Danda, Vastra |
| **Nauli** | Massages and strengthens abdominal organs through muscle contractions | Madhyamanauli, Vamanauli, Dakshinanauli, Naulikriya |
| **Basti** | Detoxifies the colon, improves digestion | Sthalabasti, Jalabasti |
| **Kapalabhati** | Cleanses frontal region of brain (lobes) and lungs through rapid breathing | — |
| **Trataka** | Focused gazing without blinking — concentration and eye health | — |

### I. Neti Kriya

Cleaning and purifying the nasal passage.

#### (a) Jalaneti — "nasal irrigation"

Rinsing the nasal cavity with salted water using a neti pot.

**Technique:**

1. Mix half a teaspoon of salt in one cup of warm water.
2. Pour the solution into the neti pot.
3. Seal the spout inside one nostril (gentle twists help).
4. Let the water flow in, then tilt the head to one side so it flows out of the other nostril.
5. Breathe through the **mouth**; neither sniff nor swallow while water flows.

**Benefits:** Reduces allergy problems • Improves breathing • Eliminates post-nasal drip • Cures sinusitis / chronic sinus infection • Improves resistance to common cold • Improves sense of smell

**Contraindications:** Practise under expert guidance • Not with persistent nose bleeding

#### (b) Sutra Neti

A waxed cotton string (or rubber catheter) is inserted through the nose, brought out through the mouth, and moved to and fro to clean the nasal passage.

**Technique:**

1. Very gently insert the thread/catheter into the left nostril.
2. Push it along the side of the nose until it touches the back of the throat.
3. Open the mouth; catch the tip with the index and middle fingers.
4. Pull it partially out of the mouth, holding the other tip too.
5. Move it gently in a massaging motion, then pull it out through the nose.
6. Repeat with the other nostril.

**Benefits:** Maintains nasal hygiene (removes dirt/bacteria trapped in mucus) • De-sensitises nasal tissue → helps rhinitis, allergies, some types of asthma • Reduces sinusitis, migraine and headaches

**Contraindications:** Expert guidance • Never force the catheter — it irritates the membrane • Try only after mastering Jalaneti

### II. Dhauti Kriya

Complete cleaning of the digestive and respiratory systems; also helps clean the eyes and ears.

#### (a) Vamana Dhauti (Kunjala)

Cleanses the respiratory and upper digestive system. Also called *Kunjala* (gesture of the elephant).

**Procedure:**

1. Dissolve 1 tablespoon salt in 1 litre lukewarm water.
2. Gulp the salt water as quickly as possible until you can't drink more.
3. Lean forward, keeping the torso horizontal.
4. Vomit out the stomach contents by inserting the middle and index fingers into the throat, until no water is left.
5. Lie down in **Shavasana** afterwards.

**Precautions:** Don't sip — drink fast, or the technique won't work • Drink a large quantity • Relaxation comes with practice

> 📝 The textbook translates *Vamana* as "middle". In Sanskrit it usually means **"vomiting"**, which matches the technique. Write it as your teacher expects.

#### (b) Danda Dhauti

A (disinfected) rubber tube is inserted down the oesophagus into the stomach; it also cleans the windpipe.

**Procedure:** Drink salt water as in Vamana Dhauti → gently insert the tube until it reaches the stomach → bend forward so the salt water oozes out → remove the tube after all the water has come out.

**Precautions:** If you can't swallow the tube, stick to Vamana Dhauti • Only under an expert yoga teacher

#### (c) Vastra Dhauti

*Vastra* = cloth. Removes mucus from the stomach; a muslin cloth is swallowed and removed after about ten minutes.

**Procedure:**

1. Use a disinfected muslin cloth, about **20 ft × 3 inches**.
2. Place it in the mouth and swallow slowly — start with 1 ft and increase gradually every day.
3. Sip warm water if the cloth dries and sticks in the throat.
4. Stop when about **6 inches** remain outside; then withdraw slowly.

**Precautions:** No synthetic cloth • No loose threads/frayed edges • Always leave some cloth outside • Cloth narrower than the tongue (so it doesn't fold) • Disinfect after use • Expert teacher must be present for beginners

**Contraindications (all Dhauti):** Hernia, ulcer, hypertension, heart disease

**Advantages of Dhauti:** Body returns to its natural state by eliminating toxins, stomach acid, bile and mucus • Helps heartburn, constipation, acidity, indigestion

### III. Nauli Kriya

Massaging and strengthening the abdominal organs. A difficult exercise mastered only through patience and perseverance.

| Variation | Focus |
| --------- | ----- |
| **Madhyamanauli** | Central abdominal muscles — isolated contraction |
| **Vamanauli** | Left portion of the abdominal muscles — isolated contraction |
| **Dakshinanauli** | Right portion of the abdominal muscles — isolated contraction |
| **Naulikriya** | Central abdominal muscles — circular movement |

**Procedure:**

1. Stand straight with legs slightly apart; take deep breaths through the nose.
2. Bend forward, exhale through the mouth, back straight; hands on thighs, knees slightly bent.
3. Draw the abdominal side muscles in to create a **suction effect**; contract the *rectus abdominis* (parallel to the centre).
4. To inhale, stand straight and take a deep breath. Repeat 5–6 times, or as long as the muscles allow.
5. After some practice, the central abdomen can be moved in different directions and in a circular movement.

**Precautions:** Only on an **empty stomach** • Avoid after abdominal surgery, during menstruation and pregnancy • Consult a doctor for intestine or pancreas disease

**Advantages:** Strong abdominal muscles, massages intestines and lower-abdomen organs • Regulates blood pressure, can prevent diabetes • Treats skin problems like acne, and heartburn • Improves digestion

### IV. Basti Kriya

Cleanses the lower abdomen, especially the **colon**. Benefits are mentioned in Hatha Yoga texts.

| Technique | Also called | Method |
| --------- | ----------- | ------ |
| **Sthalabasti** | Sushkabasti / Vatabasti | Cleans the colon by sucking air into the body, **without** catheter or tube |
| **Jalabasti** | Vatibasti | Cleans the colon by using a catheter to suck **water** into the anus |

### V. Kapalabhati Kriya

*Kapal* = skull (including all organs under it); *bhati* = shining/illuminating. A breathing technique purifying the frontal region of the brain.

**Technique:**

1. Sit comfortably in **Sukhasana**.
2. Draw the navel to the spine in a quick motion, forcefully expelling all air — the primary movement is from the **diaphragm**.
3. Let the lungs fill naturally, with no effort.
4. Do this cycle **10 times**, then let breathing return to normal and observe the sensations. Repeat 3–4 times.

**Benefits:** Improves blood circulation, gives radiance to the face • Improves digestion, absorption and assimilation • Reduces weight by increasing metabolic rate • Clears the *nadis* • Calms and uplifts the mind

**Contraindications:** Not for pregnant women • People with high blood pressure should not increase the rate

### VI. Trataka Kriya

*Trataka* = "to look, or to gaze". Intense gazing at one point/object to develop concentration and strengthen the eyes.

**Technique:**

1. Sit in Sukhasana (or any comfortable posture), waist, back and neck erect, breathing normally.
2. Place a lighted candle at **eye level**, about **2 feet** away.
3. Gaze at the flame without blinking until the eyes strain and water.
4. Close the eyes; rub the palms until warm and place them over the eyes.
5. Visualise the flame at the centre of the eyebrows.

**Benefits:** Strengthens eye muscles, relieves mild eye problems • Improves concentration, intelligence and memory • Enhances self-confidence, patience and will power • Calms the mind, gives inner peace • Brings mental clarity and better decision-making

### Benefits of Yogic Kriyas (in general)

1. Overcoming diseases
2. Creating harmony between body and mind
3. Purification of the body, leading to better energy flow
4. Improves the capacity to work, think and feel/experience

## 4. Pranayama and its Types

**Pranayama** = the practice of breath regulation. *Prana* = life energy, *Yama* = control. It is a main component of yoga and an exercise for physical and mental wellness, used for relaxation, concentration and meditation. **Patanjali** mentions pranayama as a means of attaining higher states of awareness.

**Three stages:**

| Stage | Meaning |
| ----- | ------- |
| **Puraka** | Inhale |
| **Kumbhaka** | Retain the breath |
| **Rechaka** | Exhale |

**Important pranayamas:** Anuloma-Viloma (Nadi Shodhana) • Surya Bhedi • Ujjayi • Bhramari • Sheetkari • Sheetali

> 📝 Other lists (incl. class notes) also name **Bhastrika**, **Bahya**, **Digra** and **Kapalabhati**. Anulom-Vilom and Nadi Sodhana are the *same* practice.

### Essentials for Practising Pranayama

| Essential | Requirement |
| --------- | ----------- |
| **External environment** | Well-ventilated place, free from noise, insects and flies |
| **Right time** | Most effective in the **morning** |
| **Seat** | Soft, thick and comfortable |
| **Asana** | Padmasana, Siddhasana, Vajrasana or Sukhasana |

### I. Anuloma-Viloma / Nadi-Shodhana

*Anuloma* = towards; *Viloma* = reverse. Nostrils are used alternately, in reverse order, for each inhalation and exhalation.

**Procedure:**

1. Sit in a meditative posture, head and spine erect, eyes closed.
2. Close the right nostril with the right thumb; inhale slowly through the **left**.
3. Close the left nostril with the ring and little finger; exhale slowly through the **right**.
4. Inhale through the right; close it with the thumb; exhale through the left.
5. That is **one round** — repeat 5 times.

**Benefits:** Calms and steadies the mind, improves focus • Improves blood supply to the brain • Balances left and right brain hemispheres, promotes clear thinking • Helps in asthma, allergies, high/low BP, stress-related heart conditions, hyperactivity, insomnia, chronic pain, endocrine imbalance, anxiety and stress

**Contraindications:** Safest when guided by a trained teacher • Stop if lightheaded or having trouble breathing

### II. Surya Bhedi Pranayama

A **warming** pranayama focused on the **right nostril** (*Surya* = sun, linked to *Pingala nadi*). *Bhedana* = to pierce/pass through.

**Procedure:**

1. Sit with a straight spine and neck, eyes closed, aware of the breath.
2. Fold the right index and middle fingers to the palm; ring and little fingers stay natural.
3. Close the left nostril; inhale slowly and deeply through the right; retain the breath a few seconds.
4. Close the right nostril with the thumb, open the left and exhale slowly. Repeat for 1–3 minutes.

**Benefits:** Removes Kapha imbalance by raising body temperature • Helps cure obesity • Increases vitality • Helps depression and lack of energy

**Contraindications:** Avoid in high blood pressure or heart disease

### III. Ujjayi Pranayama

*Ujjayi* = "one who is victorious" (from *ud* + *ji*) — suggests power and upliftment.

**Procedure:**

1. Sit in Sukhasana or another meditative posture, spine and neck straight.
2. Close the mouth and constrict the throat (the **glottis**, part of the larynx).
3. Make a short exhalation, then inhale slowly and rhythmically in one long, unbroken inspiration.
4. Air passing through the constricted throat makes a **hissing "friction sound"**.
5. Continue until the chest feels full; retain for about 6 seconds (ideally double the inspiration time).
6. Exhale naturally and gradually, without jerks. Take a few normal breaths and relax.

**Benefits:** Corrects fluid retention (edema/dropsy) • Clears phlegm, increases appetite • Helps chronic cold, cough, indigestion, liver problems, dysentery, fever • Enriches the voice • Stimulates and balances the thyroid

**Contraindications:** Not recommended in serious cardiac and hypertension cases

### IV. Bhramari Pranayama

Named after the black bee *Bhramari* — the exhalation resembles its humming sound.

**Procedure:**

1. Sit straight in Padmasana (or any comfortable posture), eyes closed.
2. Close the ears, eyes and lips.
3. Breathe in deeply; while breathing out make a humming sound like a bee — it needn't be loud but should create vibrations.

**Benefits:** Beneficial effect on the nervous system • Restores elasticity of lungs, good in asthmatic conditions • Induces meditative states and quietens the mind • Relieves hypertension and stress • Lowers tension and anxiety • Reduces anger and frustration

**Contraindications:** Ear infection or irritation • Recurring chest pains • High blood pressure

### V. Sheetkari / Sitkari Pranayama

*Sitkari* = "sipping" or "hissing". Calms the mind and **cools** the body.

**Procedure:**

1. Sit in Padmasana or any comfortable asana, eyes closed.
2. Touch the palate with the tongue.
3. Press the teeth together, keeping the lips open.
4. Draw air in through the mouth with a hissing sound.
5. Retain as long as you can, with **chin lock** and **anal lock**.
6. Exhale slowly through both nostrils, releasing the locks.

**Benefits:** Useful in hot weather (cooling) • Benefits nervous system and endocrine glands • Reduces excess bile • Controls hunger and thirst • Relaxes body and mind • Helps lower blood pressure

**Contraindications:** Cold and cough, asthma or other respiratory problems, tonsillitis

### VI. Sheetali / Sitali Pranayama

*Sitali* = cooling; designed to reduce body temperature and bring harmony to the body system.

**Procedure:**

1. Sit in Padmasana or another comfortable posture, hands on knees in *Jnana mudra* or *Anjali mudra*.
2. Roll the tongue from the sides into a **tube**.
3. Inhale through the tube-shaped tongue to full lung capacity; take the tongue in and close the mouth.
4. Exhale slowly through both nostrils — one round.
5. Repeat 4 more times.

**Benefits:** Purifies blood • Cools body and mind • Good for high blood pressure • Satisfies thirst, appeases hunger • Relieves indigestion and bile (*pitta*) disorders • Helps chronic dyspepsia and spleen problems • Good for skin and eyes

**Contraindications:** Severe cold, cough or tonsillitis

### Quick Comparison

| Pranayama | Key effect | Avoid if |
| --------- | ---------- | -------- |
| **Anuloma-Viloma** | Calms mind, balances brain hemispheres | Lightheaded / trouble breathing |
| **Surya Bhedi** | Warming; vitality, reduces obesity | High BP, heart disease |
| **Ujjayi** | Thyroid, clears phlegm, voice | Serious cardiac / hypertension |
| **Bhramari** | Relieves stress, anger; good for asthma | Ear infection, chest pain, high BP |
| **Sheetkari** | Cooling; controls hunger/thirst | Cold, asthma, tonsillitis |
| **Sheetali** | Cooling; purifies blood, good for BP | Severe cold, cough, tonsillitis |

## 5. Active Lifestyle and Stress Management through Yoga

Long-lasting stress can lead to physical and mental health issues. An **active lifestyle** (staying physically active regularly) reduces stress because exercise releases **endorphins** — "feel-good" hormones that improve mood and reduce anxiety, and it releases built-up tension.

**Yoga is especially beneficial** because it combines **asanas** (postures), **pranayama** (breathing) and **meditation**, which calm the mind and relax the body.

In yogic philosophy, health is **holistic** — body, mind and emotions in balance. Five Sanskrit terms (from Yoga and Ayurveda) cover how to live healthily:

| Term | Refers to | Meaning |
| ---- | --------- | ------- |
| **Ahara** | Diet | What, when, how, how much to eat and what not to eat. Fresh, whole, easy-to-digest foods. Yogic *mitahara*: **½ stomach food, ¼ water, ¼ air** |
| **Vihara** | Daily routine | Three components — relaxation, recreation, relationships. Includes sleeping pattern, exercise routine, work schedule |
| **Achara** | Conduct | Ethical and moral principles — honest, truthful, compassionate to oneself and others |
| **Vichara** | Introspection | Examining thoughts and beliefs for self-awareness; spotting behaviour patterns that hinder well-being |
| **Vyavahara** | Social behaviour | Positive relationships; empathy, compassion, being respectful, kind and helpful |

### General Guidelines for Yoga Practice

| Phase | Guidelines |
| ----- | ---------- |
| **Before** | **Saucha** — clean surroundings, body and mind • Calm, quiet space • Empty or light stomach (if weak, a little honey in lukewarm water) • Empty bladder and bowels • Use a mat/blanket/cushion • Light, comfortable cotton clothes • Don't practise when exhausted, ill, in a hurry or under acute stress • Consult a physician/yoga therapist if pregnant or with chronic pain, cardiac problems or any medical condition |
| **During** | Begin with a **prayer or invocation** • Practise **mindfully** (slow, aware of body and breath) • Breathe **naturally through the nostrils**; don't hold the breath unless told to • Avoid tension and jerking • Practise **within your limits** • Be patient and consistent • Remember each practice's limitations and contraindications • End with **meditation/silence** or the *Sankalp Shantipatha* |
| **After** | **Rest** in Shavasana • **Rehydrate** • Eat a light meal after **20–30 minutes** • Take it easy — avoid over-stimulating or stressful activity |

### Relaxation Technique for Concentration — Yoga Nidra

**Yoga Nidra** = "sleep" **with awareness** — a state between wakefulness and dream. (In normal sleep we sleep without awareness.) It helps manage stress, relaxes body and mind, and reduces tension.

Practised in **Shavasana**, it consists of **body awareness** and **breath awareness**: awareness is rotated in quick succession through all parts of the body, then taken to the breath and finally to the mind.

| Phase | What to do |
| ----- | ---------- |
| **1. Body awareness** | Lie supine in Shavasana, take a deep breath. Rotate awareness through the body and relax each part in turn: right foot (toes, sole, heel, ankle, calf, knee, thigh, hip) → left foot → right hand (fingers, palm, wrist, arm, shoulder) → left hand → back (buttocks, back, shoulder blades, spine) → front (chest, navel, abdomen, upper legs) → neck, face and head (chin, lips, nose, cheeks, ears, eyes, eyebrows, head) → whole body |
| **2. Breath awareness** | Feel the natural flow of breath *without changing it*; feel the nostrils, then the abdomen rising and falling with each breath |
| **3. Coming back** | Become aware of body, time and place; move fingers, toes and head side to side; take your time, open the eyes slowly, sit up |

**Remember:** Relax parts one by one • Focus on the part being named • Follow instructions properly • Don't tense the muscles • **Do not sleep** • Best on an empty stomach

**Contraindications:** Not for people with severe clinical depression or other challenging mental health conditions • Not while driving or operating machinery (you may fall asleep)

**Benefits of Yoga Nidra:**

| # | Benefit | How |
| - | ------- | --- |
| 1 | **Reduces stress** | Builds inner strength and the ability to cope; stay calm and balanced |
| 2 | **Trains the mind** | A *sankalpa* (positive intention) is planted in the calm, receptive mind at the start of the session — it must be clear, strong and full of belief |
| 3 | **Relaxes the mind** | Moving awareness through the body stimulates the brain, promotes deep relaxation and clears nerve pathways → calmness and clarity |
| 4 | **Clears the unconscious** | Observing images/symbols with detached awareness brings hidden emotions and past impressions to the surface and lets them go |
| 5 | **Awakens creativity** | Links the conscious and unconscious mind → creative and intuitive abilities |
| 6 | **Improves memory & learning** | Activates both sides of the brain; more alertness and concentration — especially useful for students |
| 7 | **Manages psychological disorders** | Releases deep-rooted tension; trains the mind to stay calm (anxiety, depression) |
| 8 | **Manages psychosomatic diseases** | Stress/conflict/frustration appearing as physical problems is released through deep relaxation |

## 6. Asanas for Lifestyle Diseases

Yoga plays a vital role in preventing and managing **lifestyle diseases** caused by poor diet, lack of physical activity and stress. Two asanas are prescribed for each major lifestyle disease.

### Overview

| Disease | Asana 1 | Asana 2 |
| ------- | ------- | ------- |
| **Obesity** | Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation) | Trikonasana (Triangle Pose) |
| **Diabetes** | Dhanurasana (Bow Pose) | Ardha Matsyendrasana (Half Spinal Twist) |
| **Hypertension** | Shavasana (Corpse Pose) | Pawanmuktasana (Wind-Relieving Pose) |
| **Back Pain** | Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose) | Makarasana (Crocodile Pose) |
| **Asthma** | Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose) | Gomukhasana (Cow Face Pose) |

### A. Obesity

#### 1. Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation)

- **Procedure:** A sequence of 12 postures performed in a flow, synchronised with breathing — Pranamasana → Hasta Uttanasana → Hasta Padasana → Ashwa Sanchalanasana → Dandasana → Ashtanga Namaskara → Bhujangasana → Adho Mukha Svanasana → and reverse back to the start.
- **Benefits:** Burns calories, improves metabolism, tones the whole body.
- **Contraindications:** High blood pressure, hernia, pregnancy, recent surgery.

#### 2. Trikonasana (Triangle Pose)

- **Procedure:** Stand with legs wide apart; stretch one arm down towards the foot and the other up, forming a triangle with the body. Hold, then repeat on the other side.
- **Benefits:** Stretches the sides of the body, stimulates digestion, reduces fat around the waist.
- **Contraindications:** Neck/back injury, low blood pressure, migraine.

### B. Diabetes

#### 1. Dhanurasana (Bow Pose)

- **Procedure:** Lie on the stomach, bend the knees, hold the ankles with the hands, lift the chest and thighs off the ground to form a bow.
- **Benefits:** Stimulates the pancreas, improves insulin secretion, strengthens the back.
- **Contraindications:** Hernia, high/low blood pressure, back injury, pregnancy.

#### 2. Ardha Matsyendrasana (Half Spinal Twist)

- **Procedure:** Sit with one leg bent, twist the torso towards the bent-knee side, place the opposite elbow outside the knee, look over the shoulder.
- **Benefits:** Massages the abdominal organs including the pancreas, improves digestion, stimulates insulin production.
- **Contraindications:** Spinal injuries, pregnancy, peptic ulcer.

### C. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

#### 1. Shavasana (Corpse Pose)

- **Procedure:** Lie flat on the back, arms and legs relaxed and slightly apart, eyes closed; breathe slowly and deeply.
- **Benefits:** Deep relaxation, reduces stress and blood pressure, calms the nervous system.
- **Contraindications:** Generally safe for all; avoid falling asleep for too long during practice.

#### 2. Pawanmuktasana (Wind-Relieving Pose)

- **Procedure:** Lie on the back, bring one or both knees to the chest, clasp the hands around the knees, press gently, hold and release.
- **Benefits:** Relieves gas, relaxes the body, helps lower blood pressure.
- **Contraindications:** Hernia, recent abdominal surgery, pregnancy.

### D. Back Pain

#### 1. Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)

- **Procedure:** Lie on the stomach, palms under the shoulders, slowly lift the chest upward keeping the hips on the ground.
- **Benefits:** Strengthens the spine, relieves back stiffness, improves posture.
- **Contraindications:** Pregnancy, hernia, recent back/spine surgery.

#### 2. Makarasana (Crocodile Pose)

- **Procedure:** Lie on the stomach, fold the arms and rest the forehead/chin on them, relax the whole body, breathe deeply.
- **Benefits:** Relaxes the spine, relieves lower back pain, reduces stress.
- **Contraindications:** Generally safe; avoid with severe abdominal discomfort.

### E. Asthma

#### 1. Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)

- **Procedure:** Same as above (see Back Pain).
- **Benefits (for asthma):** Expands the chest, improves lung capacity and breathing.
- **Contraindications:** Same as above.

#### 2. Gomukhasana (Cow Face Pose)

- **Procedure:** Sit with the knees stacked one over the other; arms behind the back with fingers interlocked (one arm over the shoulder, the other from below).
- **Benefits:** Opens the chest and shoulders, improves lung capacity, expands the respiratory muscles.
- **Contraindications:** Shoulder or knee injury.
