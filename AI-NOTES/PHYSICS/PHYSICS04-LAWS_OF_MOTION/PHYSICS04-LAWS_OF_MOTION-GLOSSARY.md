# Physics | Chapter 04 | Laws of Motion | GLOSSARY
> **Key Terms** | Board · NEET · JEE

---

> All terms are arranged **alphabetically**. Each entry gives a precise definition, its defining relation and dimensional formula where they apply, and a `§` pointer to the NOTES section that explains it.

---

## A

**Action and Reaction**
The two forces of a Third-Law pair: the force on A by B and the equal and opposite force on B by A, either of which may be called the action. They act simultaneously on *different* bodies, so they never cancel each other (forces that cancel act on the same body). *§4.1–4.2*

**Angle of Friction (λ)**
The angle between the normal to the surface and the resultant of the normal force and the limiting static friction, so tan λ = (fs)max/N = μs. It equals the angle of repose. *§8.5*

**Angle of Repose (θr)**
The steepest angle of incline at which a body rests on the surface without sliding. Resolving forces (N = mg cosθ, fs = mg sinθ, fs ≤ μsN) gives tan θr = μs, independent of the body's mass. *§8.5*

**Apparent Weight**
The normal force N that a supporting surface exerts on a body, which is what a weighing scale reads; it equals the weight mg only when the body has no vertical acceleration. For an upward acceleration a, N − mg = ma gives N = m(g + a); for a downward acceleration a, N = m(g − a). *§10.2*

**Aristotle's Fallacy**
The incorrect belief that a net force is needed to keep a body moving; it overlooks friction and air resistance, which are what bring an unpushed body to rest. *§1.1*

**Atwood Machine**
Two masses m₁ > m₂ joined by an ideal string over an ideal pulley. Applying F = ma to each mass gives a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂) and T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂). *§10.3*

---

## B

**Banked Road (Banking Angle θ)**
A curved road whose surface tilts toward the centre of the curve (outer edge higher) by the angle θ, so that a component of the normal force points toward the centre. *§9.3*

---

## C

**Centripetal Force (fc)**
The net inward radial force required for motion on a circle of radius R at speed v, of magnitude mv²/R; it is not an extra force but the name for whichever real forces (tension, gravity, friction, normal force) supply it. Dimensional formula: [MLT⁻²]. *§9.1*

**Coefficient of Kinetic Friction (μk)**
The dimensionless constant, characteristic of a pair of sliding surfaces, in fk = μkN; for a given pair it is generally less than μs. *§8.3*

**Coefficient of Static Friction (μs)**
The dimensionless constant, characteristic of a pair of surfaces at rest relative to each other, in (fs)max = μsN; it equals tan θr. *§8.2*

**Conservation of Momentum**
The total momentum of an isolated system is constant (pA + pB = pA′ + pB′ for two bodies); it follows from the Second and Third Laws and holds in both elastic and inelastic collisions. It also holds component-wise: along any direction in which the net external force is zero, the momentum component is conserved. *§5.1–5.2*

**Contact Force**
A force that acts through the physical contact of two bodies (normal force, friction, tension, spring force); at the molecular level all contact forces arise from electrical forces between the charged constituents of matter. *§7.5*

---

## E

**Elastic and Inelastic Collision**
In an elastic collision the total kinetic energy of the colliding bodies is unchanged; in an inelastic collision it is not. Total momentum is conserved in both. *§5.2*

**Equilibrium of a Particle**
The state in which the net external force on a particle is zero (ΣF = 0), so its acceleration is zero: it is at rest (static equilibrium) or moves with constant velocity (dynamic equilibrium). *§6.1*

**External Force**
A force on a system exerted by a body outside the system; only external forces can change the system's total momentum. *§3.3, §5.2*

---

## F

**Force (F)**
An interaction that changes, or tends to change, a body's state of rest or uniform motion; quantitatively F = dp/dt, which is ma for constant mass. It is a vector with SI unit newton and dimensional formula [MLT⁻²]. *§2.2, §3.2*

**Free-Body Diagram (FBD)**
A diagram of one chosen body, isolated from its surroundings, showing only the external forces acting on it as vectors; forces the body exerts on other bodies are not drawn. *§6.3*

**Free Fall**
Motion under the gravitational force alone (air resistance neglected), with downward acceleration g independent of the body's mass and apparent weight zero. *§10.2*

**Friction**
The component of the contact force parallel to the surfaces in contact that opposes actual or impending relative motion between them. It is not a fundamental force, its laws are empirical, and its kinds are static, kinetic and rolling. *§8.1*

## G

**Gravitational Force**
See **Weight**.

## H

**Hooke's Law**
For small extensions, the force a spring exerts is proportional to the extension x from its natural length and opposite to it: F = −kx (a restoring force). It fails once the spring is stretched beyond the point where it no longer returns to its natural length. *§7.4*

---

## I

**Ideal String and Pulley**
A massless string over a massless, frictionless pulley: the tension is then the same throughout the string. If the string is also inextensible, the bodies it connects have equal speed and acceleration along it. *§7.3*

**Impulse (J)**
The integral of force over the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum: J = ∫F dt = Δp (J = FΔt for a constant or average force); it is a change in momentum, not momentum itself. SI unit: N s (= kg m s⁻¹); dimensional formula: [MLT⁻¹]. *§3.4*

**Impulsive Force**
A large force acting for a very short time, whose effect is measured by the impulse it delivers (Δp = F_avg Δt); it is not a separate kind of force. *§3.4*

**Inertia**
The property of a body by which it resists any change in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line; its measure is mass. *§1.2*

**Inertial Frame of Reference**
A frame in which a body acted on by no net force moves with constant velocity; any non-rotating frame in uniform straight-line motion relative to it is also inertial. Newton's laws in their usual form hold only in such frames. *§2.2*

**Internal Force**
A force between two parts of the same system; internal forces occur in Third-Law pairs and sum to zero, so they cannot change the system's total momentum. *§4.2, §5.2*

**Isolated System**
A system on which the net external force is zero, so its total momentum stays constant. *§5.2*

---

## K

**Kinetic Friction (fk)**
Friction between surfaces sliding relative to one another: fk = μkN, directed opposite to the relative sliding. Unlike static friction, whose value adjusts to the situation, it has the fixed value μkN, nearly independent of sliding speed and contact area. *§8.3*

---

## L

**Lami's Theorem**
For three concurrent forces (lines of action meeting at a point) in equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two: F₁/sin α = F₂/sin β = F₃/sin γ, with α between F₂ and F₃, β between F₃ and F₁, γ between F₁ and F₂. *§6.5*

**Law of Inertia**
Galileo's conclusion that a body needs no net force to continue at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line, drawn from the limit of ever-smaller resistive forces. Newton's First Law states it precisely. *§1.2*

**Limiting Friction**
The maximum static friction, (fs)max = μsN, that a pair of surfaces can supply; if more would be needed to prevent sliding, the body slides. *§8.2*

---

## M

**Mass (m)**
The measure of a body's inertia: a given net force produces an acceleration inversely proportional to the mass (a = F/m). It is a scalar with SI unit kg and, unlike weight, does not vary with location. *§1.2, §3.2*

**Momentum (p)**
The product of a body's mass and velocity, p = mv; a vector in the direction of the velocity, with SI unit kg m s⁻¹ (= N s) and dimensional formula [MLT⁻¹]. Its change over a time interval equals the impulse. *§3.1*

---

## N

**Net External Force**
The vector sum of all external forces acting on a body or system; it alone determines the acceleration (F = ma), and internal forces are excluded. *§3.3*

**Newton (N)**
The SI unit of force: the force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻², so 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻². *§3.2*

**Newton's First Law**
A body continues at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless a net external force acts on it: ΣF = 0 ⟹ a = 0 in an inertial frame. Its content beyond the Second Law is that inertial frames exist. *§2.1–2.2*

**Newton's Second Law**
The rate of change of a body's momentum equals the net external force on it and is in the direction of that force: F = dp/dt, which is ma for constant mass. It is a vector law (one equation per axis) and a local one (the force at an instant fixes the acceleration at that instant). *§3.2–3.3*

**Newton's Third Law**
The force on A by B is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force on B by A, FAB = −FBA; the two act simultaneously on different bodies. *§4.1*

**Normal Force (N)**
The component of the contact force perpendicular to the surfaces in contact; it is always a push and is self-adjusting up to what the surface can bear. It is not always mg, and it is not the Third-Law partner of the weight, since both act on the same body. *§7.2*

---

## O

**Optimum Speed (v₀)**
On a banked road, the speed at which no friction is needed: N cosθ = mg and N sinθ = mv₀²/R give v₀ = √(Rg tanθ). At v₀ no sideways friction acts on the tyres. *§9.3*

---

## P

**Pseudo Force**
An extra force that must be added in an accelerating (non-inertial) frame for Newton's laws to hold there; it comes from no interaction with another body and is not used in this chapter. *§2.2*

---

## R

**Reaction**
See **Action and Reaction**.

**Recoil**
The backward motion of a gun when it fires a projectile; conservation of momentum gives v_gun = −(m_bullet/m_gun) v_bullet, with velocities measured relative to the ground. *§5.3*

**Rolling Friction**
The small resistance to rolling caused by slight deformation of the rolling body and the surface at the contact, much smaller than kinetic (sliding) friction. In ideal rolling without slipping, no sliding friction acts. *§8.6*

---

## S

**Self-Adjusting Force**
A force whose magnitude automatically takes whatever value the situation requires, up to a maximum; static friction (up to μsN) and the normal force (up to what the surface can bear) are examples. *§7.2, §8.2*

**Spring Constant (k)**
The stiffness of a spring in F = −kx, the force per unit extension; a larger k means a stiffer spring. SI unit: N m⁻¹; dimensional formula: [MT⁻²]. *§7.4*

**Static Friction (fs)**
Friction that opposes impending relative motion, present whenever there is a tendency to slide; it is self-adjusting, 0 ≤ fs ≤ μsN, with maximum (fs)max = μsN. Unlike kinetic friction, its value is set by the situation, not fixed by N. *§8.2*

---

## T

**Tension (T)**
The force transmitted along a string, rope or cable under stretch; it pulls on the body attached and is directed from the body toward the string, and a string cannot push. *§7.3*

---

## W

**Weight (W)**
The gravitational force of the Earth on a body near its surface, W = mg, directed vertically downward; unlike mass it varies with location, and it differs from apparent weight, which is the normal force. Dimensional formula: [MLT⁻²]. *§7.1*

**Weightlessness**
The state of zero apparent weight (N = 0), as in free fall: gravity still acts, but the body and its surroundings fall with the same acceleration g, so the surface exerts no force on the body. *§10.2*
