# Physics | Chapter 07 | Gravitation | GLOSSARY

### Gravitation

---

## A

**Aphelion**
The point in a planet's elliptical orbit farthest from the Sun, where orbital speed is minimum (by angular momentum conservation, $r_p v_p = r_A v_A$). For Earth this distance is ≈1.52×10¹¹ m. *§2.1, §2.2*

**Apogee**
The point in a satellite's orbit around Earth farthest from Earth's centre, where orbital speed is minimum — the Earth-orbit analog of aphelion, which is reserved for orbits around the Sun. *See Aphelion; §8.1*

---

## B

**Binding Energy (Satellite)**
The minimum energy needed to remove a satellite from its orbit to infinity, equal to the magnitude of its total mechanical energy: $|E| = GM_Em/[2(R_E+h)]$. *§9.4*

---

## C

**Cavendish Experiment**
Henry Cavendish's 1798 torsion-balance measurement of $G$: a bar of small lead spheres twists under the pull of two large spheres until the gravitational torque balances a known restoring torque, giving $G = 6.67\times10^{-11}$ N m² kg⁻². Knowing $G$ then lets $M_E$ itself be calculated from $g$ and $R_E$ — commonly called "weighing the Earth." *§4.1*

**Central Force**
A force always directed along the line joining two interacting bodies, which makes the torque about the source zero and angular momentum conserved. Gravity is a central force, which is why Kepler's Law of Areas holds for it — and for any central force in general. *§2.2*

**Circular Orbit**
The limiting case of an elliptical orbit with zero eccentricity, where both foci coincide at the centre. At radius $r$: orbital speed $V=\sqrt{GM_E/r}$, time period $T=2\pi r/V$. *§2.1, §8.1*

**Copernican Model**
See **Heliocentric Model**. *§1.1*

---

## E

**Eccentricity (e)**
A measure of how elongated an ellipse is: $e=0$ for a circle, $0<e<1$ for an ellipse, $e=1$ for a parabola (an open, unbound path). Earth's orbital eccentricity ≈0.0167 — nearly circular. *§2.1*

**Ellipse**
A closed curve for which the sum of distances from any point on it to two fixed foci is constant: $TF_1+TF_2=2a$, where $a$ is the semi-major axis. Kepler's First Law states all planetary orbits are ellipses, with a circle as the special case where the two foci coincide. *§2.1*

**Escape Speed ($v_e$)**
The minimum surface launch speed needed to reach infinity with zero leftover speed: $v_e=\sqrt{2GM_E/R_E}\approx11.2$ km s⁻¹ for Earth. It depends only on the planet's mass, radius, and launch height — never on the escaping body's own mass or launch direction. *§7.1, §7.3*

---

## F

**Focus (Foci)**
One of two fixed points defining an ellipse. In Kepler's First Law the Sun occupies one focus of each planet's orbit; the other focus is empty, and for a circle both foci coincide at the centre. *§2.1*

**Free Fall**
Motion under gravity alone with no other force acting, so every part of a body shares the same acceleration and experiences no internal stress — this is what makes a freely falling body weightless. An orbiting satellite and its occupants are in perpetual free fall: falling toward Earth while moving sideways fast enough to keep missing it. *§9.6*

---

## G

**Geocentric Model**
Ptolemy's (~150 AD) model placing Earth at the centre of the solar system, with all other bodies revolving around it using circles-within-circles (epicycles) to match observed retrograde motion. Superseded by the heliocentric model. *§1.1*

**Geostationary Satellite**
An Earth satellite in a circular equatorial orbit with a 24-hour time period matching Earth's rotation, so it appears fixed above one point on the equator. Orbit height ≈35,800 km above the surface. *§8.3*

**Gravitational Constant (G)**
The universal proportionality constant in Newton's Law of Gravitation, $F=Gm_1m_2/r^2$, with value $6.67\times10^{-11}$ N m² kg⁻² and dimensional formula $[\text{M}^{-1}\text{L}^3\text{T}^{-2}]$. It is the same for every pair of masses anywhere in the universe, first measured by Cavendish. *§3.2, §4.1*

**Gravitational Field Intensity (g)**
The gravitational force per unit mass at a point, $\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{F}/m=GM/r^2$, directed toward the source mass; a vector with dimensional formula $[\text{LT}^{-2}]$. At Earth's surface, $g\approx9.8$ m s⁻². *§5.1*

**Gravitational Potential (U)**
The gravitational potential energy per unit mass at a point, $U(r)=-GM_E/r$ (J kg⁻¹) — a scalar, always negative, with $U=0$ at infinity. Dimensional formula: $[\text{L}^2\text{T}^{-2}]$. *§6.3*

**Gravitational Potential Energy (V)**
The work done in bringing mass $m$ from infinity to distance $r$ from a source mass: $V(r)=-GM_Em/r$, always negative with $V=0$ at infinity by convention. Near Earth's surface ($h\ll R_E$), this reduces to the familiar $\Delta V\approx mgh$. *§6.1, §6.2*

**Gravity**
The attractive pull a large mass (such as Earth) exerts on smaller masses near it — Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation, applied to the everyday case of one very large body and one small one nearby. At Earth's surface it produces the acceleration $g\approx9.8$ m s⁻² directed toward Earth's centre. *§3.2, §5.1*

---

## H

**Heliocentric Model**
The model placing the Sun at the centre of the solar system, with planets orbiting it — first hinted at by Aryabhatta, formalized with circular orbits by Copernicus, corrected to ellipses by Kepler, and explained by Newton's law. Also called the Copernican Model. *§1.1*

---

## I

**Inverse-Square Law**
The statement that gravitational force falls off as $1/r^2$ with distance — the form Newton deduced by comparing the Moon's centripetal acceleration to $g$ at Earth's surface (ratio 3600 = $(R_M/R_E)^2$). Several other physical laws (electrostatic force, light intensity) also follow an inverse-square form. *§3.1*

---

## K

**Kepler's Laws**
Three empirical laws of planetary motion — elliptical orbits (First Law), equal areas in equal times (Second Law), and $T^2\propto a^3$ (Third Law) — extracted by Kepler from Tycho Brahe's observations. They apply equally to satellite orbits around Earth, with Earth replacing the Sun. *§2.1–§2.3*

---

## N

**Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation**
Every pair of masses in the universe attracts each other with force $F=Gm_1m_2/r^2$, directed along the line joining them, obeying Newton's Third Law ($\mathbf{F}_{12}=-\mathbf{F}_{21}$). This single law unifies terrestrial gravity with celestial motion and explains all three of Kepler's Laws. *§3.2*

---

## O

**Orbital Speed**
The tangential speed a satellite needs to maintain a stable circular orbit at height $h$: $V=\sqrt{GM_E/(R_E+h)}$, decreasing as $h$ increases. At Earth's surface ($h\approx0$), $V_0\approx7.9$ km s⁻¹. *§8.1*

---

## P

**Perigee**
The point in a satellite's orbit around Earth closest to Earth's centre, where orbital speed is maximum — the Earth-orbit analog of perihelion. *See Perihelion; §8.1*

**Perihelion**
The point in a planet's elliptical orbit closest to the Sun, where orbital speed is maximum. For Earth this distance is ≈1.47×10¹¹ m. *§2.1, §2.2*

**Principle of Superposition (Gravitation)**
The total gravitational force on a mass due to several others is the vector sum of the individual forces, each acting as if the others weren't present. The same superposition applies to potential energy: total PE is the sum over all pairs. *§3.3*

**Ptolemaic Model**
See **Geocentric Model**. *§1.1*

---

## R

**Radius of Orbit**
For a satellite in circular orbit, the distance from Earth's centre to the satellite: $r=R_E+h$, where $h$ is height above Earth's surface. *§8.1*

---

## S

**Semi-Major Axis (a)**
Half the longest diameter of an ellipse, $a=(r_p+r_A)/2$ for a planetary orbit — the quantity in Kepler's Third Law ($T^2\propto a^3$). For a circular orbit, $a$ equals the radius. *§2.1*

**Shell Theorem**
Two results for a uniform spherical shell: it attracts an external point mass exactly as if all its mass were concentrated at its centre, and it exerts zero force on any point mass inside it. This lets Earth be modeled as concentric shells when finding $g$ above and below its surface. *§3.4*

---

## T

**Tidal Force**
The difference in gravitational pull on the near and far sides of a body due to a nearby massive object, depending on the *gradient* of gravity ($dF/dr\propto1/r^3$) rather than on $F$ itself. This is why the Moon's tides exceed the Sun's despite the Sun's overall pull on Earth being stronger — the Moon is close enough for a much steeper gradient. *Points to Ponder #8 — not derived in a numbered NOTES section*

**Time Period (Orbital)**
The time for one full satellite revolution, $T=2\pi(R_E+h)^{3/2}/\sqrt{GM_E}$, increasing with orbital height. For low Earth orbit, $T_0\approx85$ min; for a geostationary orbit, $T=24$ h. *§8.2*

---

## U

**Universal Gravitation**
Newton's insight that the same force causing objects to fall on Earth also governs the Moon's orbit and all celestial motion — "universal" meaning it applies to any two masses anywhere, regardless of composition or what lies between them. *§3.2*

---

## V

**Virial Theorem (Gravitational)**
For a bound object in a stable circular orbit, kinetic and potential energy are locked in fixed proportion: $KE=-\tfrac{1}{2}PE=-E$, so $|PE|=2KE$ and the total energy $E$ is always negative. *§9.3*

---

## W

**Weightlessness**
The condition where a body exerts no force on its support and a scale reads zero, occurring when both the body and its support are in free fall together — **not** when gravity is absent. An orbiting astronaut is weightless because the spacecraft and astronaut fall toward Earth at the same rate, leaving no supporting (normal) force between them. *§9.6*

## Contrast Table — Key Pairs That Students Confuse

| Aspect | Circular Orbit | Escape (to infinity) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Energy condition | $E < 0$ (bound) | $E \geq 0$ (free) |
| Required speed | $V_0 = \sqrt{gR_E} \approx 7.9$ km s⁻¹ | $v_e = \sqrt{2gR_E} \approx 11.2$ km s⁻¹ |
| Ratio | — | $v_e/V_0 = \sqrt{2}$ |
| Orbit shape | Closed circle/ellipse | Open parabola or hyperbola |

| Aspect | g Above Surface | g Below Surface |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Formula | $g(h) \approx g_0(1-2h/R_E)$ | $g(d) = g_0(1-d/R_E)$ |
| Variation | Decreases as $1/(R_E+h)^2$ | Decreases linearly with d |
| At extremity | $g \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$ | $g = 0$ at Earth's centre |
| Rate for small displacement | Factor of $2h/R_E$ | Factor of $d/R_E$ |

## Common Misconceptions Clarified

| Misconception | Correct Understanding |
|:---|:---|
| Astronauts in space are weightless because there is no gravity | Gravity at orbital altitude is ~90% of surface value; weightlessness is due to free fall — astronaut and spacecraft fall together |
| Escape speed depends on the mass or direction of the projectile | Escape speed is independent of both; it depends only on the planet's mass, radius, and launch height |
| A satellite in higher orbit moves faster | The opposite — higher orbit means lower orbital speed ($V \propto 1/\sqrt{r}$) |
| Gravitational PE is zero at Earth's surface | By convention, GPE = 0 at infinity; GPE at any finite distance is negative |
| Supplying energy to a satellite speeds it up | Supplying energy to raise orbit moves it to a higher orbit with LOWER speed (less KE, more PE) |
| Moon causes weaker tides than the Sun because it's smaller | Tidal force depends on the gradient $dF/dr\propto1/r^3$; the Moon is much closer, so its gradient — and tidal effect — is steeper |
| $g = 0$ everywhere inside Earth | $g = 0$ only at Earth's centre; it falls linearly from $g_0$ at the surface to 0 at the centre |
| Kepler's 2nd Law is unique to gravitation | The law of areas holds for ANY central force; it is equivalent to conservation of angular momentum |
