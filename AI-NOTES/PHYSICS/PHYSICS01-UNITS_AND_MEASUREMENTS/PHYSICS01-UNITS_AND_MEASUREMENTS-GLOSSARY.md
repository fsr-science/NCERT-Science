# Physics | Chapter 01 | Units and Measurements | GLOSSARY
> **Units and Measurements** | Alphabetical lookup. Each entry is a stand-alone definition with a § back-reference to NOTES.

---

## A

> [!note] **Absolute error**
> The magnitude of the difference between one reading and the mean of the readings, $\Delta a_i = |a_i - \bar{a}|$, in the unit of the quantity. Relative error, by contrast, is a dimensionless ratio. *§2.6.3*

> [!note] **Accuracy**
> Closeness of a measured value to the true value. Precision is agreement among repeated readings, so readings can be precise yet inaccurate (for example, with a zero error). *§2.6.1*

> [!note] **Ampere (A)**
> SI base unit of electric current, defined by fixing the elementary charge at exactly $e = 1.602176634 \times 10^{-19}$ C (where $1 \text{ C} = 1 \text{ A s}$). *§1.3*

> [!note] **Ångström (Å)**
> Non-SI unit of length, $1 \text{ Å} = 10^{-10}$ m, used for atomic and molecular sizes. *§1.5*

> [!note] **Arithmetic mean**
> The sum of $n$ repeated readings divided by $n$, $\bar{a} = (a_1 + \cdots + a_n)/n$; it is the best estimate of the true value. Averaging reduces random error but not systematic error. *§2.6.3*

> [!note] **Astronomical unit (AU)**
> Non-SI unit of length equal to the mean Earth–Sun distance, $1 \text{ AU} \approx 1.496 \times 10^{11}$ m. *§1.5*

## B

> [!note] **Base quantity**
> One of the independent quantities chosen as the foundation of a system of units, from which all other quantities are built; SI has seven: length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity. *§1.1*

> [!note] **Base unit**
> The unit of a base quantity; the SI base units are metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela. The quantity is the property measured (length); the unit is the standard it is measured against (metre). *§1.1, §1.3*

> [!note] **BIPM**
> International Bureau of Weights and Measures (Sèvres, France), which maintains the international measurement standards. It maintains SI; the CGPM decides it. *§1.2*

## C

> [!note] **Candela (cd)**
> SI base unit of luminous intensity, defined by fixing the luminous efficacy (luminous flux per unit power) of radiation of frequency $540 \times 10^{12}$ Hz at exactly $683 \text{ lm W}^{-1}$. *§1.3*

> [!note] **CGPM**
> General Conference on Weights and Measures, the international body that adopts and revises SI. It decides SI; the BIPM maintains the standards. *§1.2*

> [!note] **CGS system**
> System of units whose base units of length, mass and time are the centimetre, gram and second. *§1.2*

## D

> [!note] **Derived quantity**
> A quantity defined from one or more base quantities through a definition or law, such as speed (length / time) or force (mass × acceleration). *§1.1*

> [!note] **Derived unit**
> The unit of a derived quantity, obtained by combining base units; some carry special names, such as the newton, $1 \text{ N} = 1 \text{ kg m s}^{-2}$. *§1.1, §4.2*

> [!note] **Dimensional analysis**
> The technique of treating dimensions as algebraic quantities, with the principle of homogeneity, to check an equation, deduce a relation between quantities, or convert units. It can reject an equation but never prove one correct. *§5*

> [!note] **Dimensional equation**
> An equation equating a physical quantity to its dimensional formula, for example $[F] = [MLT^{-2}]$. The dimensional formula is only the bracketed expression on the right. *§4.2*

> [!note] **Dimensional formula**
> The expression giving the exponents of the base quantities in a physical quantity, for example $[ML^2T^{-2}]$ for energy. The exponents themselves are the quantity's dimensions. *§4.1*

> [!note] **Dimensionless quantity**
> A quantity whose dimensional formula has every exponent zero, $[M^0L^0T^0]$, such as angle, strain or refractive index. It may still have a unit (angle: radian), so dimensionless is not the same as unitless. *§3.4*

> [!note] **Dimensions**
> The exponents to which the base quantities must be raised to represent a physical quantity, written in square brackets. They describe its nature independent of units, not its geometric size. *§3.1*

## E

> [!note] **Error**
> The difference between a measured value and the true value of a quantity; it is a deviation, not necessarily a mistake. *§2.6*

> [!note] **Exact number**
> A number with no uncertainty, either counted (the number of readings) or defined (the $2$ in $2\pi r$). It has unlimited significant figures and never limits the precision of a result. *§2.2*

## F

> [!note] **Fermi (fm)**
> Unit of length, $1 \text{ fm} = 10^{-15}$ m (one femtometre), used for nuclear sizes. *§1.5*

> [!note] **FPS system**
> System of units whose base units of length, mass and time are the foot, pound and second (the British system). *§1.2*

## G

> [!note] **Gross error**
> A blunder such as misreading a scale or recording a wrong value, removed by repeating the measurement and discarding the faulty reading. Unlike systematic and random errors, it is a mistake rather than a property of the instrument or method. *§2.6.2*

## H

> [!note] **Homogeneity, principle of**
> Only quantities with identical dimensions can be added, subtracted or equated, so every term of a valid equation has the same dimensions. A failed check means the equation is definitely wrong; a passed check means only that it is possibly correct. *§5.1*

## I

> [!note] **International System of Units (SI)**
> The internationally accepted system of units, built on seven base units and adopted and revised by the CGPM. *§1.2*

## K

> [!note] **Kelvin (K)**
> SI base unit of thermodynamic temperature, defined by fixing the Boltzmann constant at exactly $k = 1.380649 \times 10^{-23} \text{ J K}^{-1}$. *§1.3*

> [!note] **Kilogram (kg)**
> SI base unit of mass, defined by fixing the Planck constant at exactly $h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}$ (where $\text{J s} = \text{kg m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}$). Mass prefixes attach to the gram, not the kilogram. *§1.3, §1.6*

## L

> [!note] **Least count**
> The smallest value an instrument can read directly; it sets the uncertainty of a single reading and therefore the instrument's precision. *§2.1*

> [!note] **Light year (ly)**
> The distance light travels in vacuum in one year, $9.46 \times 10^{15}$ m. It is a unit of distance, not of time. *§1.5*

## M

> [!note] **Mean absolute error**
> The average of the absolute errors of all readings, $\Delta\bar{a} = (\Delta a_1 + \cdots + \Delta a_n)/n$; a result is reported as $\bar{a} \pm \Delta\bar{a}$. *§2.6.3*

> [!note] **Metre (m)**
> SI base unit of length, defined by fixing the speed of light in vacuum at exactly $c = 299{,}792{,}458 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ (with the second already defined). *§1.3*

> [!note] **MKS system**
> System of units whose base units of length, mass and time are the metre, kilogram and second; the precursor of SI. *§1.2*

> [!note] **Mole (mol)**
> SI base unit of amount of substance; one mole contains exactly $6.02214076 \times 10^{23}$ elementary entities (the Avogadro constant $N_A$). The kind of entity (atoms, molecules, ions) must always be stated. *§1.3*

## O

> [!note] **Order of magnitude**
> The power of ten nearest to a quantity: for $N \times 10^n$ with $1 \le N < 10$ it is $n$ if $N \le 5$ and $n + 1$ if $N > 5$ (NCERT convention). Unlike scientific notation, it discards the coefficient. *§2.3*

## P

> [!note] **Parsec (pc)**
> The distance at which 1 AU subtends an angle of 1 arcsecond (1/3600 of a degree), $3.086 \times 10^{16}$ m $\approx 3.26$ light years. *§1.5*

> [!note] **Percentage error**
> Relative error expressed as a percentage, $(\Delta\bar{a}/\bar{a}) \times 100\%$. Relative error itself has no factor of 100. *§2.6.3*

> [!note] **Physical quantity**
> A property that can be measured, expressed as a numerical value multiplied by a unit. *§1.1*

> [!note] **Precision**
> Closeness of repeated measurements to one another, limited by the instrument's least count. A precise set of readings need not be accurate. *§2.6.1*

> [!note] **Prefix (SI)**
> A symbol placed before a unit symbol to multiply it by a power of ten (kilo $= 10^3$, milli $= 10^{-3}$). The prefix is raised to a power together with the unit, so $1 \text{ km}^2 = 10^6 \text{ m}^2$. *§1.6*

## R

> [!note] **Radian (rad)**
> SI unit of plane angle, $\theta = s/r$ (arc length over radius), so it is dimensionless. A full circle is $2\pi$ rad. *§1.4*

> [!note] **Random error**
> An error that varies unpredictably in sign and size from reading to reading, reduced by averaging many readings. A systematic error, by contrast, has a fixed direction and is not reduced by averaging. *§2.6.2*

> [!note] **Relative error**
> The ratio of the mean absolute error to the mean value, $\Delta\bar{a}/\bar{a}$, which is dimensionless. Multiplied by 100% it becomes the percentage error. *§2.6.3*

> [!note] **Rounding off**
> Reducing a number to fewer digits by comparing the dropped part with half a unit of the last kept place: more than half raises the preceding digit, less than half leaves it. An exact 5 leaves an even preceding digit unchanged and raises an odd one. *§2.5*

## S

> [!note] **Scientific notation**
> Writing a number as $N \times 10^n$ with $1 \le N < 10$; every digit of $N$ is significant. *§2.3*

> [!note] **Second (s)**
> SI base unit of time, defined by fixing the frequency of the unperturbed ground-state hyperfine transition of caesium-133 at exactly $9{,}192{,}631{,}770$ Hz. *§1.3*

> [!note] **Significant figures (SF)**
> All digits of a measured value that are known with certainty, plus the first uncertain digit. The count does not change with the unit: $2.308$ cm and $0.02308$ m both have 4 SF. *§2.1*

> [!note] **Steradian (sr)**
> SI unit of solid angle, $\Omega = A/r^2$ (intercepted area over radius squared), so it is dimensionless. A full sphere is $4\pi$ sr. *§1.4*

> [!note] **Supplementary units**
> NCERT's name for the radian and steradian, both dimensionless; the SI has classed them as dimensionless derived units since 1995. **Source flag:** use the NCERT term in exam answers. *§1.4*

> [!note] **System of units**
> A complete set of base units together with the derived units built from them. *§1.1*

> [!note] **Systematic error**
> An error of fixed direction and size in every reading, from causes such as zero error, poor calibration or parallax. It is not reduced by averaging; it is removed by calibration or correction. *§2.6.2*

## T

> [!note] **True value**
> The actual value of a quantity; in practice estimated by the mean of many careful readings. *§2.6*

## U

> [!note] **Unified atomic mass unit (u)**
> Non-SI unit of mass equal to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom, $1 \text{ u} = 1.66054 \times 10^{-27}$ kg, used for atomic and molecular masses. *§1.5*

> [!note] **Unit**
> The agreed reference standard against which a physical quantity is measured. Different units (metre, foot) can measure quantities of the same dimension. *§1.1*
