# Physics | Chapter 09 | Mechanical Properties of Fluids | GLOSSARY

### Mechanical Properties of Fluids

---

> All terms arranged **alphabetically**, one to two sentences each, with dimensional formulae where applicable and a `§` back-reference to the NOTES section that derives them. For a term's exam priority, see the ⭐ rating on that NOTES section heading — it is the single priority signal for the chapter. Terms drawn only from the handwritten class notes carry **(Supp.)**.

---

## A

**Absolute Pressure** — The total pressure at a point in a fluid measured from a perfect vacuum, $P=P_a+\rho gh$ at depth $h$ in an open liquid. It equals the gauge pressure plus atmospheric pressure, and is never negative. *(§3.3, §3.5)*

**Adhesive Force** — The attractive force between molecules of **different** substances, such as water and glass; compare cohesive force, which acts between molecules of the *same* substance. *(§9.1)*

**Aerofoil** — A solid body shaped, like an aircraft wing cross-section, so that its motion through a fluid crowds the streamlines above it more than below, producing an upward dynamic lift. *(§7.5)*

**Angle of Contact (θ)** — The angle, measured inside the liquid, between the tangent to the liquid surface at the point of contact and the solid surface. It satisfies $S_{la}\cos\theta+S_{sl}=S_{sa}$, and is acute for a wetting liquid and obtuse for a non-wetting one. *(§9.4)*

**Atmosphere (atm)** — A unit of pressure equal to the sea-level atmospheric pressure, $1\ \text{atm}=1.013\times10^5$ Pa $=76$ cm of mercury. *(§2.2)*

**Atmospheric Pressure ($P_a$)** — The pressure at a point due to the weight of the column of air of unit cross-section above it; about $1.013\times10^5$ Pa at sea level. Measured with a mercury barometer. *(§4.1)*

---

## B

**Bar** — A unit of pressure, $1\ \text{bar}=10^5$ Pa, slightly less than 1 atm; used in meteorology, where $1$ millibar $=100$ Pa. *(§2.2)*

**Barometer (Mercury)** — A device, invented by Torricelli, that measures atmospheric pressure by the height $h$ of a mercury column in an inverted closed tube, $P_a=\rho gh$ with $h\approx76$ cm at sea level. *(§4.1)*

**Bernoulli's Equation (Bernoulli's Principle)** — Along a streamline in steady, non-viscous, incompressible flow, $P+\tfrac12\rho v^2+\rho gh$ stays constant: it is conservation of energy per unit volume. It is *not* the equation of continuity, which is conservation of mass. *(§7.1)*

---

## C

**Capillary Rise** — The height $h=\dfrac{2S\cos\theta}{\rho ga}$ to which a liquid climbs in a narrow tube of radius $a$ when the contact angle is acute; if the angle is obtuse, $h$ is negative and the liquid is depressed instead. *(§9.6)*

**Coefficient of Viscosity (η)** — The ratio of shearing stress to strain rate in a fluid, $\eta=\dfrac{F/A}{v/l}$. SI unit Pa s (poiseuille); dimensional formula **$[ML^{-1}T^{-1}]$**. *(§8.2)*

**Cohesive Force** — The attractive force between molecules of the **same** substance (water–water, mercury–mercury); compare adhesive force, which acts between *different* substances. *(§9.1)*

**Compressibility** — The extent to which the volume of a substance changes under a change in pressure; it is high for gases and low for liquids and solids, which is why liquids are treated as incompressible. *(§1.3)*

**Critical Speed (Critical Velocity)** — The limiting flow speed below which a flow is steady (streamline) and above which it becomes turbulent; it is $v_c=R_{e,c}\,\eta/(\rho D)$ for flow in a tube of diameter $D$. *(§6.3, §8.5)*

---

## D

**Density (ρ)** — Mass per unit volume, $\rho=m/V$; a positive scalar. SI unit kg m⁻³; dimensional formula **$[ML^{-3}]$**. *(§2.3)*

**Dynamic Lift** — The force on a body, such as a wing, a hydrofoil or a spinning ball, that arises from pressure differences produced by its motion through a fluid. *(§7.5)*

---

## E

**Efflux** — The outflow of a fluid through an opening; the speed of efflux from an open tank at depth $h$ is $\sqrt{2gh}$ (Torricelli's law). *(§7.3)*

**Equation of Continuity** — For steady flow of an incompressible fluid, $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$: the volume flux $Av$ is constant along a tube of flow. It expresses conservation of mass, and is a different law from Bernoulli's equation. *(§6.4)*

**Excess Pressure** — The amount by which the pressure inside a curved liquid–gas interface exceeds that outside: $2S/r$ for a drop or an air bubble in a liquid (one surface), $4S/r$ for a soap bubble (two surfaces). *(§9.5)*

---

## F

**Fluid** — A substance that can flow and has no shape of its own; liquids and gases are both fluids. A fluid offers very little resistance to shear stress. *(§1.1)*

**Fluid Dynamics (Hydrodynamics)** — The branch of physics that deals with fluids in motion. *(§1.2, §6.1)*

**Fluid Statics (Hydrostatics)** — The branch of physics that deals with fluids at rest. *(§1.2)*

---

## G

**Gauge Pressure** — The excess of the actual pressure over atmospheric pressure, $P_g=P-P_a=\rho gh$ at depth $h$; it is what tyre gauges, blood-pressure instruments and an open manometer read. Compare absolute pressure, which includes $P_a$. *(§3.3, §3.5)*

---

## H

**Hydraulic Brakes** — A braking system in which pedal force on a small master piston creates a pressure that the brake oil transmits equally to larger pistons at all four wheels, giving equal braking force on every wheel. *(§5.4)*

**Hydraulic Lift** — A device in which a small force $F_1$ on a piston of area $A_1$ supports a large force $F_2=F_1A_2/A_1$ on a piston of area $A_2$, by Pascal's law. It multiplies force but not work. *(§5.1, §5.3)*

**Hydrostatic Paradox** — The fact that vessels of different shapes and volumes joined at the bottom fill to the *same* level, because pressure at a given depth depends only on $\rho$, $g$ and $h$. *(§3.5)*

---

## L

**Laminar Flow (Streamline Flow)** — Smooth flow in which fluid layers slide over one another along parallel paths without mixing; it occurs below the critical speed (for $R_e$ below about 2000 in a pipe). Compare turbulent flow. *(§6.3, §8.5)*

---

## M

**Magnus Effect** — The sideways (here, upward) force on a spinning body moving through a fluid, caused by the air speed being larger on one side of the ball than the other. *(§7.5)*

**Manometer (Open-Tube)** — A U-tube containing a liquid, one end open to the atmosphere and the other connected to the system; the height difference $h$ gives the gauge pressure, $P-P_a=\rho gh$. *(§4.3)*

**Mechanical Advantage (Hydraulic)** — The factor $A_2/A_1$ by which a hydraulic lift multiplies the applied force. *(§5.1)*

**Meniscus** — The curved free surface of a liquid in contact with a solid wall; concave for a wetting liquid (adhesion greater than cohesion), convex for a non-wetting one, and plane when they are equal (Supp.). *(§9.4)*

---

## P

**Pascal (Pa)** — The SI unit of pressure, $1\ \text{Pa}=1\ \text{N m}^{-2}$; dimensional formula **$[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$**. *(§2.1)*

**Pascal's Law** — The pressure in a fluid at rest is the same in all directions at a point and the same at all points at one level; a pressure change applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every part of it. *(§3.1)*

**Poise** — The CGS unit of viscosity, equal to $0.1$ Pa s; the SI unit, the poiseuille, is therefore $10$ poise (called a "decapoise" in the handwritten notes). *(§8.2)*

**Poiseuille (Pl)** — The SI unit of viscosity, $1\ \text{Pl}=1\ \text{Pa s}=1\ \text{N s m}^{-2}$. *(§8.2)*

**Pressure (P)** — The normal force per unit area, $P=F/A$; a **scalar** with no direction of its own. SI unit Pa; dimensional formula **$[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$**. *(§2.1)*

---

## R

**Relative Density (Specific Gravity)** — The ratio of a substance's density to the density of water at $4^\circ$C; a dimensionless positive scalar. *(§2.3)*

**Reynolds Number ($R_e$)** — The dimensionless ratio $R_e=\rho vD/\eta$ that decides the nature of flow in a pipe: streamline below about 2000, unstable between 2000 and 3000, turbulent above 3000 (Supp.). *(§8.5)*

---

## S

**Steady Flow** — Flow in which, at any given point, the velocity of every fluid particle that passes remains constant in time; the velocity may still differ from point to point. *(§6.1)*

**Stokes' Law** — The viscous drag on a small sphere of radius $a$ moving at speed $v$ through a fluid of viscosity $\eta$ is $F=6\pi\eta av$, opposite to the motion. *(§8.4)*

**Streamline** — The path of a fluid particle in steady flow: a curve whose tangent at every point is along the fluid velocity there. Two streamlines never cross. *(§6.2)*

**Surface Energy** — The extra energy per unit area possessed by molecules at a liquid's surface compared with those in its interior; it equals the work done in increasing the surface area by unit amount. Numerically it equals the surface tension. *(§9.2)*

**Surface Tension (S)** — The force per unit length acting in the plane of a liquid interface, equal to the surface energy per unit area, $S=F/2l$ for a two-faced film. SI unit N m⁻¹; dimensional formula **$[MT^{-2}]$**. The symbol $\sigma$ means surface tension in the handwritten notes but fluid density in NCERT's Stokes' law; these notes use $S$ and $\sigma$ as NCERT does. *(§9.2)*

---

## T

**Terminal Velocity ($v_t$)** — The constant speed reached by a body falling through a viscous fluid when weight equals buoyant force plus viscous drag; for a sphere $v_t=\dfrac{2a^2(\rho-\sigma)g}{9\eta}$. *(§8.4)*

**Torr** — A unit of pressure equal to $1$ mm of mercury, $1\ \text{torr}=133$ Pa; used in medicine and physiology. *(§2.2)*

**Torricelli's Law** — The speed of efflux from a small hole at depth $h$ below the surface of an open tank is $v=\sqrt{2gh}$, the speed of a body in free fall through $h$. *(§7.3)*

**Turbulent Flow** — Irregular flow, above the critical speed, in which the path and velocity of the fluid change continuously with whirlpools; Bernoulli's equation does not apply to it. Compare laminar flow. *(§6.3, §7.2)*

---

## V

**Viscosity** — The property of a fluid by virtue of which an internal frictional force acts between adjacent layers in relative motion and opposes that motion. It is measured by the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$; for liquids it falls, and for gases it rises, with temperature. *(§8.1, §8.3)*

**Viscous Force** — The tangential, backward-dragging force between layers of a fluid in relative motion, $F=\eta A\,v/l$ for a layer of area $A$. *(§8.1, §8.2)*

**Volume Flux (Flow Rate)** — The volume of fluid crossing a section per unit time, $Q=Av$. SI unit m³ s⁻¹; dimensional formula **$[L^3T^{-1}]$**. *(§6.4)*

---

## W

**Wetting Agent** — A substance (soap, detergent, dye) that, when added to a liquid, reduces its angle of contact with a solid so the liquid penetrates well; a waterproofing agent does the opposite. *(§9.4)*
