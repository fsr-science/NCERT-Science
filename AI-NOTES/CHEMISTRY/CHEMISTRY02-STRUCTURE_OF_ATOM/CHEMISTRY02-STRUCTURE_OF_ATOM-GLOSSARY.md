# Chemistry | Chapter 02 | Structure of Atom | GLOSSARY

### Structure of Atom

---

> Terms arranged **alphabetically**. Each entry is a short, airtight definition — for full derivations, worked examples, and context, follow the `§` reference back into NOTES. Exam tags mark applicability, not difficulty.

---

## A

**Absorption Spectrum**
Dark lines on a continuous bright background, produced when white light passes through an unexcited gas and specific wavelengths are absorbed. The absorbed wavelengths exactly match the gas's emission wavelengths — its photographic negative. *(Board/NEET)* §5.1

**Angular Momentum Quantum Number (l)**
Also called the azimuthal or subsidiary quantum number; fixes the **shape** of an orbital and its subshell. For a given n, l = 0 to (n−1): l=0(s), l=1(p), l=2(d), l=3(f). *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §9.2

**Angular Node**
A plane (or cone) through the nucleus where |ψ|² = 0. Number of angular nodes = **l**. Example: 2p has 1; 3d has 2. Contrast: Radial Node is spherical, not planar. *(NEET/JEE)* §8.4

**Atomic Number (Z)**
Number of protons in the nucleus; equals electron count in a neutral atom. Z alone identifies the element, regardless of isotope. *(Board/NEET)* §2.4

**Atomic Orbital**
The wave function ψ for one electron — a 3D region of ~90% probability, defined by (n, l, mₗ), holding at most 2 electrons. Distinct from Bohr's "orbit," which is a defined path, not a probability region. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §8.2

**Aufbau Principle**
*(German: "building up")* — ground-state electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy, per the (n+l) rule (lower n+l first; ties broken by lower n). Cr and Cu are exceptions (§14.1). *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §12.1

---

## B

**Balmer Series**
Hydrogen lines ending at n=2, the only series in the **visible** region. First line (n=3→2) is red at 656 nm; discovered empirically by Balmer, 1885. *(Board/NEET)* §5.3

**Black Body**
An ideal absorber/emitter of all frequencies uniformly; a cavity with a small hole approximates one. Its emission spectrum depends only on temperature, and defeated classical wave theory until Planck's quantum explanation. *(Board/NEET)* §4.1

**Bohr Model**
Niels Bohr's 1913 model for hydrogen: electrons occupy fixed circular "stationary states" with quantised angular momentum, mₑvr = nh/2π. Explains H's stability and spectrum; fails for multi-electron atoms and contradicts Heisenberg. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §6.1

**Bohr Radius (a₀)**
Radius of the n=1 orbit in hydrogen: **a₀ = 52.9 pm = 0.529 Å**. General: rₙ = n²a₀; for H-like species, rₙ = 52.9n²/Z pm. *(Board/NEET)* §6.2

**Boundary Surface Diagram**
A surface enclosing the ~90% probability region of an orbital — never 100%, since |ψ|² approaches but never reaches exactly zero at any finite distance. Its shape is the orbital's shape (sphere, dumbbell, cloverleaf). *(Board/NEET)* §10.1

---

## C

**Canal Rays**
Positively charged rays passing backward through holes in the cathode; charge-to-mass ratio depends on the gas used (unlike cathode rays). The lightest, from hydrogen, is the proton. *(Board)* §1.4

**Cathode Rays**
Negatively charged particles (electrons) streaming cathode→anode; e/m ratio (1.7588×10¹¹ C/kg) is independent of electrode material or gas — proof electrons are universal to all matter. Measured by Thomson, 1897. *(Board/NEET)* §1.1–1.2

**Continuous Spectrum**
An unbroken band of all wavelengths (e.g. ROYGBIV), produced when white light passes through a prism. Forms the bright background of an absorption spectrum. *(Board)* §5.1

---

## D

**de Broglie Relation**
Every moving particle has an associated wavelength: **λ = h/mv = h/p**. Negligible for macroscopic objects; ~10⁻¹⁰ m for electrons — measurable, confirmed by electron diffraction. *(NEET/JEE)* §7.1

**Degenerate Orbitals**
Orbitals of equal energy. All orbitals with the same n are degenerate in hydrogen only; in multi-electron atoms this splits by l (s<p<d<f), though orbitals within one subshell (e.g. the three 2p) stay degenerate. *(NEET/JEE)* §11.1–11.2

**Deuterium (²H or D)**
Hydrogen's second isotope: 1 proton, 1 neutron; 0.015% natural abundance. Same chemistry as protium, different physical properties due to mass. *(Board)* §2.5

**Dual Nature (Wave–Particle Duality)**
Radiation (and matter) shows wave behaviour (diffraction, interference) and particle behaviour (photoelectric effect) depending on the experiment. Proposed for light by Einstein; extended to matter by de Broglie. *(Board/NEET)* §4.3, §7.1

---

## E

**Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)**
Net positive charge an outer electron actually feels, after inner-electron shielding: **Zeff = Z − σ**. Within a shell, Zeff order is s > p > d > f, since s electrons penetrate closest to the nucleus. *(NEET/JEE)* §11.2

**Electron**
Fundamental negatively charged particle; charge −1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C, mass 9.109×10⁻³¹ kg. Number in a neutral atom = Z; determines chemical behaviour. *(Board/NEET)* §1.1–1.3

**Electronic Configuration**
Arrangement of electrons in orbitals, written as subshell (1s²2s²2p⁶) or noble-gas notation ([Ne]3s¹), built via Aufbau → Pauli → Hund in that order. Cr and Cu break the expected pattern. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §13.1

**Emission Spectrum**
Bright coloured lines on a dark background, produced when excited atoms fall to lower energy states. Each element's pattern is a unique fingerprint used in spectroscopic identification. *(Board/NEET)* §5.1

**Exchange Energy**
Energy released when same-spin electrons in degenerate orbitals swap positions — a stabilising effect, maximum at half-filled (d⁵) or fully-filled (d¹⁰) subshells. The quantum basis for Hund's rule and the Cr/Cu exceptions. *(JEE)* §14.1

**Excited State**
Any state of an atom above the ground state — one or more electrons occupying a higher-energy orbital than the lowest available. Reached by absorbing energy; decays back toward the ground state by emitting a photon. *(Board/NEET)* §6.2

---

## G

**Ground State**
An atom's lowest-energy, most stable configuration, filled per the standard rules. For hydrogen: n=1, E₁ = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸ J. Contrast: Excited State. *(Board/NEET)* §6.2

---

## H

**Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle**
Position and momentum of a microscopic particle cannot both be known exactly: **Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π**. A property of nature, not instrument error — and the reason a defined Bohr orbit is physically meaningless. *(NEET/JEE)* §7.2

**Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity**
Degenerate orbitals are singly filled, with parallel spins, before any pairing — maximising exchange energy. Example: carbon's 2p² is ↑ ↑ in separate orbitals, not ↑↓ paired. *(Board/NEET)* §12.3

**Hydrogen-Like Species**
Any atom/ion with exactly one electron regardless of Z: He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺. Bohr's formulas apply with Z scaling: Eₙ = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸(Z²/n²) J, rₙ = 52.9n²/Z pm. *(NEET/JEE)* §6.2

---

## I

**Isobars**
Atoms of **different elements** sharing the same mass number A (same nucleon count). Example: ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N (A=14; Z=6 and 7). Contrast: Isotopes (same Z). *(Board/NEET)* §2.5

**Isotones**
Atoms sharing the same neutron count, (A−Z), regardless of element or mass number. Completes the isotope/isobar/isotone trio that exam questions commonly test as "identify the pair." *(NEET)* §2.5

**Isotopes**
Atoms of the **same element** (same Z) with different mass number A, i.e. different neutron counts. Identical chemistry, different physical properties — disproving Dalton's "all atoms of an element are identical." *(Board/NEET)* §2.5

---

## L

**Line Spectrum (Atomic Spectrum)**
Discrete, specific wavelengths only (not a continuous range) — bright lines in emission, dark in absorption. Arises because electrons occupy only quantised energy levels. *(Board/NEET)* §5.1

**Lyman Series**
Hydrogen lines ending at n=1, in the **ultraviolet**; highest energy of all the series. Series limit (n=∞→1) at ~91 nm. *(Board/NEET)* §5.3

---

## M

**Magnetic Orbital Quantum Number (mₗ)**
Fixes an orbital's spatial orientation. For given l: mₗ = −l to +l, i.e. **(2l+1)** values — matching the orbital count in that subshell. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §9.3

**Mass Number (A)**
Total nucleons (protons + neutrons): **A = Z + neutrons**. Always a whole number for a given nuclide — unlike a periodic-table atomic mass, which is an isotope-weighted average. *(Board/NEET)* §2.4

---

## N

**Neutron**
Electrically neutral nucleon, mass 1.675×10⁻²⁷ kg, discovered by Chadwick (1932) via α-bombardment of beryllium. Count = A − Z; distinguishes isotopes. *(Board/NEET)* §1.5

**Node (Nodal Surface)**
Any region where |ψ|² = 0 — zero electron probability. Total nodes = n−1, split between radial (spherical) and angular (planar) nodes. See Radial Node, Angular Node for each type's formula. *(NEET/JEE)* §8.4

**Nuclear Model (Rutherford's)**
1911 model: dense positive nucleus at the centre, electrons circling it like planets, held by electrostatic attraction. Explains where mass/charge sit, but not why electrons don't spiral in (§2.6). *(Board/NEET)* §2.3

**Nucleon**
Collective term for protons and neutrons; total nucleon count = mass number A. *(Board)* §1.6

---

## O

**Orbit (Bohr)**
A well-defined circular path of fixed radius, characterised only by n. Contradicts Heisenberg (implies exact position and velocity simultaneously); replaced by the orbital in the quantum-mechanical model. Contrast: Atomic Orbital. *(Board/NEET)* §6.1

**Orbital Penetration**
How close an electron's probability density approaches the nucleus despite orbiting at a given n. Governs shielding effectiveness: s penetrates closest, then p, then d — the root cause of Zeff order within a shell. *(NEET/JEE)* §11.2

---

## P

**Pauli Exclusion Principle**
No two electrons share all four quantum numbers — equivalently, an orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with opposite spins. Sets subshell capacity at 2(2l+1) and shell capacity at 2n². *(Board/NEET)* §12.2

**Photoelectric Effect**
Light of sufficient frequency ejects electrons from a metal instantly: **hν = hν₀ + ½mₑv²**. KE depends on frequency, not intensity; intensity only sets the electron count. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §4.2

**Photon**
A discrete packet of EM energy: E = hν, p = h/λ, rest mass zero. A more intense beam has more photons, not more energetic ones. *(Board/NEET)* §4.3

**Planck's Constant (h)**
**h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s** — the constant linking a photon's energy to its frequency; appears in E=hν, λ=h/mv, Δx·Δp≥h/4π, and mₑvr=nh/2π alike. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §4.1

**Planck's Quantum Theory**
Max Planck's 1900 postulate: energy is emitted/absorbed only in discrete multiples of a quantum, **E = nhν**, not continuously — the idea that explained black-body radiation and later the photoelectric effect. *(Board/NEET)* §4.1

**Principal Quantum Number (n)**
Fixes the shell, and hence primarily the size and energy, of an orbital: n = 1,2,3… For hydrogen, Eₙ = −R_H/n² depends on n alone. *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §9.1

**Probability Density (|ψ|²)**
The square of the wave function at a point — always positive, interpreted (Born) as probability per unit volume of finding the electron there. Not itself a probability; multiply by a volume element to get one. *(NEET/JEE)* §8.2

**Protium (¹H)**
Ordinary hydrogen: 1 proton, 0 neutrons, 99.985% of natural hydrogen. The unlabelled baseline against which Deuterium and Tritium are "heavy." *(Board)* §2.5

**Proton**
Positively charged nucleon, charge equal in magnitude to the electron's, mass ≈1836× an electron's. Characterised by Rutherford (1919) as the lightest positive ion from hydrogen canal rays. *(Board/NEET)* §1.4

---

## Q

**Quantum Mechanics**
The physics of microscopic particles showing both wave and particle behaviour, built on the Schrödinger equation. Reduces to classical mechanics at macroscopic scales. *(NEET/JEE)* §8.1

**Quantum Number**
One of four numbers (n, l, mₗ, ms) that together uniquely describe an electron's state; no two electrons in an atom may share all four (Pauli). *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §9

---

## R

**Radial Node**
A spherical surface (centred on the nucleus) where ψ=0. Number = **n − l − 1**. Example: 3s has 2; 3p has 1; 3d has 0. Contrast: Angular Node is planar, not spherical. *(NEET/JEE)* §8.4

**Rydberg Constant (R_H)**
**R_H = 109,677 cm⁻¹ (wavenumber) = 2.18×10⁻¹⁸ J (energy)**. Empirical value for hydrogen's spectral lines, later derived theoretically from Bohr's model — confirming it. *(NEET/JEE)* §5.2

**Rydberg Equation**
General formula for every hydrogen spectral line: **ν̄ = R_H(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)**. Each fixed n₁ generates one named series (Lyman n₁=1, Balmer n₁=2, etc). *(Board/NEET/JEE)* §5.2

---

## S

**Schrödinger Equation**
Quantum mechanics' fundamental equation: **ĤΨ = EΨ**. Solved for hydrogen, it reproduces Bohr's energy levels and additionally yields correct 3D orbital shapes — something Bohr's model never provided. *(NEET/JEE)* §8.1

**Shielding Effect (Screening Effect)**
Inner electrons partially block outer electrons from the full nuclear charge, lowering the Zeff they feel. Same-shell order: s shields best, then p, then d — because s penetrates closest to the nucleus. *(NEET/JEE)* §11.2

**Spin Quantum Number (ms)**
Describes intrinsic electron spin; only two values, **ms = +½ or −½**. Two electrons sharing an orbital must have opposite spin — a direct Pauli consequence. *(Board/NEET)* §9.4

**Stationary State**
A Bohr orbit in which an electron's energy stays constant with no radiation — energy changes only via a jump between states, emitting/absorbing ΔE = hν. *(Board/NEET)* §6.1

---

## T

**Threshold Frequency (ν₀)**
Minimum frequency needed to eject a photoelectron at all; below it, no emission occurs regardless of intensity. Threshold energy = work function, W₀ = hν₀. *(Board/NEET)* §4.2

**Thomson's Model**
J.J. Thomson's 1898 "plum pudding" model: a uniform positive sphere with electrons embedded throughout. Explained neutrality; disproved by Rutherford's scattering results. *(Board/NEET)* §2.1

**Tritium (³H or T)**
Hydrogen's third, radioactive isotope: 1 proton, 2 neutrons; half-life ~12.3 years. Chemically identical to protium and deuterium. *(Board)* §2.5

---

## V

**Valence Electrons**
Electrons in an atom's outermost shell (highest n); determine chemical behaviour. Contrast: Core Electrons, which sit in completely filled inner shells and take no part in bonding. *(Board/NEET)* §14.2

---

## W

**Wave Function (ψ)**
A mathematical solution of the Schrödinger equation describing an electron's state; ψ itself carries no physical meaning, but |ψ|² does (see Probability Density). *(NEET/JEE)* §8.1

**Wavenumber (ν̄)**
Reciprocal of wavelength, **ν̄ = 1/λ**, typically in cm⁻¹; the unit the Rydberg equation is conventionally expressed in. *(Board/NEET)* §3.1

**Work Function (W₀)**
Minimum energy to eject an electron from a metal surface: **W₀ = hν₀**. Any photon energy above W₀ becomes the ejected electron's kinetic energy. *(Board/NEET)* §4.2

---

## Z

**Zeeman Effect**
Spectral lines split into closely spaced components under an external magnetic field — orbitals sharing n and l but different mₗ, normally degenerate, gain slightly different energies. A direct failure case for Bohr's model. *(NEET/JEE)* §6.3
