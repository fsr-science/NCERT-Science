# Chemistry | Chapter 01 | Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry | GLOSSARY
> **Alphabetical Glossary** | Board · NEET · JEE

---

## A

> [!info] **Accuracy** *(Board/NEET)*
> The degree to which a measured value agrees with the **true or accepted value** of the quantity. Independent of precision — an accurate result is not required to be precise, and a precise result is not guaranteed to be accurate. §5.3
>
> *Contrast with: Precision (closeness of repeated measurements to each other, regardless of the true value)*

---

> [!note] **Alchemy**
> A pre-scientific tradition that sought to convert base metals into gold and find the elixir of life. Chemistry developed from alchemical traditions (1300–1600 CE). In India, it existed alongside Iatrochemistry. §1.1

---

> [!note] **Atom**
> The smallest particle of an element that retains the chemical identity of that element. Proposed as 'Paramānu' by Acharya Kanda (600 BCE) and formally defined by Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808), which treated atoms as indivisible — a claim later shown false by the discovery of protons, neutrons, and electrons. Atoms may exist singly (Na, Cu) or bonded into molecules (H₂, O₂). §7

---

> [!note] **Atomic Mass** *(Board/NEET)*
> The mass of an atom expressed in **atomic mass units (u)**. Currently referenced to ¹²C = 12 u exactly (standard adopted in 1961). In practice, the **average atomic mass** is quoted, since most elements exist as a mix of isotopes. §8.1

---

> [!note] **Atomic Mass Unit (amu / u)** *(All levels)*
> A unit of mass equal to **1/12 the mass of one ¹²C atom**.
>
> $$1 \text{ u} = 1 \text{ amu} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24} \text{ g}$$
>
> The modern preferred symbol is **u**; older texts use 'amu' for the same unit. §8.1

---

> [!important] **Average Atomic Mass** *(NEET/JEE)*
> A weighted average of the masses of all naturally occurring isotopes of an element, weighted by their fractional abundances. This — not any single isotope's mass — is the value printed on the periodic table. §8.2
>
> $$\bar{A} = \sum_i (\text{fractional abundance}_i \times \text{atomic mass}_i)$$

---

> [!important] **Avogadro's Constant (Nₐ)** *(All levels)*
> The number of elementary entities in **one mole** of a substance. §9.1
>
> $$N_A = 6.02214076 \times 10^{23} \text{ mol}^{-1}$$
>
> Named in honour of Amedeo Avogadro, though he did not calculate its value himself.

---

> [!note] **Avogadro's Law** *(Board/NEET)*
> Proposed in 1811: equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. Explained Gay Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes and distinguished atoms from molecules (e.g. H₂, O₂ as diatomic). §6

---

## B

> [!note] **Balanced Chemical Equation**
> A chemical equation in which the number of atoms of each element is the same on both the reactant and product sides, satisfying the Law of Conservation of Mass. Only **coefficients** are changed to balance — subscripts are never altered, since changing a subscript changes the identity of the substance. §12.3

---

> [!note] **Bhasma**
> Metal preparations described in the *Charaka Samhita* and used in Ayurvedic medicine. Modern analysis shows bhasmas contain metal **nanoparticles** — an early, unintentional application of what is now called nanotechnology. §1.2

---

## C

> [!note] **Candela (cd)** *(Board)*
> The SI base unit of **luminous intensity**. Defined via the luminous efficacy of monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 × 10¹² Hz. §4.2

---

> [!note] **Chemical Property**
> A property that can only be observed when a substance undergoes a **chemical change** — the substance is consumed or transformed into something new in the process of observing it. Examples: combustibility, acidity, reactivity with acids. §4.1
>
> *Contrast with: Physical Property (observed without changing the substance's identity)*

---

> [!note] **Compound** *(All levels)*
> A pure substance formed when two or more elements combine chemically in a **fixed, definite ratio by mass**. Its properties differ from those of its constituent elements, and its components can be separated only by chemical methods. §3.3
>
> *Contrast with: Mixture (variable composition, separable by physical methods)*

---

> [!note] **Concentration**
> The amount of solute present in a given quantity of solution or solvent. Expressed as molarity, molality, mole fraction, or mass per cent, depending on which quantities (volume of solution, mass of solvent, or total moles) are known. §13

---

## D

> [!important] **Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808)** *(All levels)*
> Published in *'A New System of Chemical Philosophy'*. Postulates: atoms are indivisible; atoms of the same element are identical in mass and properties; compounds form from fixed ratios of atoms; atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed, in chemical reactions. §7
>
> > ⚠️ Could not explain Gay Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes, isotopes, or isobars, and gave no account of why atoms combine.

---

> [!note] **Density** *(Board/NEET)*
> Mass per unit volume of a substance. §4.5
>
> $$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$
>
> | Unit system | Unit |
> |:---:|:---:|
> | SI | kg m⁻³ |
> | Lab | g cm⁻³ or g mL⁻¹ |
>
> Higher density = particles more closely packed.

---

> [!note] **Dimensional Analysis** *(All levels)*
> Also called the **factor label method** or **unit factor method**. Converts between units by multiplying a quantity by one or more **unit factors** (ratios equal to 1) that cancel the unwanted unit and introduce the desired one. §5.4

---

## E

> [!note] **Element** *(All levels)*
> A pure substance consisting of only **one type of atom**. Cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means. May exist as single atoms (Na, Fe) or as molecules of that one element (H₂, O₂, P₄, S₈). §3.3

---

> [!note] **Elixir of Life**
> A mythical substance alchemists sought, believed to grant immortality. Along with the Philosopher's Stone, it was a primary motivation for early chemical investigation. §1.1

---

> [!important] **Empirical Formula** *(NEET/JEE)*
> The chemical formula showing the **simplest whole-number ratio** of atoms of each element in a compound, derived from % composition data. It may or may not match the molecular formula. §11
>
> Example: CH₂O is the empirical formula for both formaldehyde (CH₂O) and glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆ = 6 × CH₂O).
>
> *Contrast with: Molecular Formula (the actual atom count, always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula)*

---

## F

> [!note] **Faience**
> A type of glass made by the Harappan civilisation, used in ornaments — evidence of early glass-making in ancient India. §1.3

---

> [!important] **Formula Mass** *(Board/NEET)*
> Used instead of molecular mass for **ionic compounds** (e.g. NaCl) that exist as an extended 3D lattice rather than discrete molecules. Calculated as the sum of atomic masses over one formula unit — the smallest repeating ratio the lattice is built from. §8.4
>
> Example: NaCl = 23.0 (Na) + 35.5 (Cl) = **58.5 u**

---

## G

> [!important] **Gay Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes (1808)** *(Board/NEET)*
> Gases combine or are produced in a chemical reaction in a **simple ratio by volume**, provided all gases are at the same temperature and pressure. Not explained by Dalton's Atomic Theory; correctly explained by Avogadro's Law three years later. §6

---

## H

> [!note] **Heterogeneous Mixture** *(Board)*
> A mixture whose composition is **not uniform** throughout — different components may be visibly distinct. Example: soil, sand + salt, oil + water. §3.3
>
> *Contrast with: Homogeneous Mixture (uniform composition)*

---

> [!note] **Homogeneous Mixture** *(Board)*
> A mixture whose composition is **uniform throughout** — also called a **solution**. Example: air, sugar solution, vinegar, alloys. §3.3
>
> *Contrast with: Heterogeneous Mixture (non-uniform composition)*

---

## I

> [!note] **Iatrochemistry**
> A branch of chemistry (1300–1600 CE) focused on applying chemistry to medicine; developed alongside Alchemy and preceded modern chemistry. §1.1

---

> [!important] **Isobars** *(NEET/JEE)*
> Atoms of **different elements** (different atomic number) that happen to share the **same mass number**. The reverse pairing to isotopes: different identity, coincidentally equal mass — not a fixed ratio of neutrons to protons. Along with isotopes, a case Dalton's Atomic Theory cannot account for. §7
>
> *Contrast with: Isotopes (same element, different mass number)*

---

> [!important] **Isotopes** *(NEET/JEE)*
> Atoms of the **same element** (same atomic number) with **different mass numbers**, arising from different numbers of neutrons. §7 / §8.2
>
> Example: ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C are all isotopes of carbon.
>
> > ⚠️ Their existence is a limitation of Dalton's Atomic Theory, which assumed all atoms of one element have identical mass.
>
> *Contrast with: Isobars (different elements, same mass number)*

---

## K

> [!note] **Kelvin (K)** *(Board)*
> The SI base unit of **thermodynamic temperature**. §4.6
>
> $$K = °C + 273.15$$
>
> Has no negative values — absolute zero (0 K) is the lower bound, equal to −273.15°C.

---

> [!note] **Kilogram (kg)** *(Board/All)*
> The SI base unit of **mass**, currently defined via the fixed numerical value of Planck's constant, $h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34}$ J s. §4.2 / §4.3
>
> *Contrast with: Weight (the gravitational force on that mass, not the mass itself — see Weight vs. Mass)*

---

## L

> [!important] **Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier, 1789)** *(All levels)*
> In all physical and chemical changes there is no net change in mass — matter is neither created nor destroyed. Forms the basis for balancing chemical equations. §6

---

> [!important] **Law of Definite Proportions (Proust)** *(All levels)*
> A given compound always contains the same elements combined in the same fixed proportion by mass, regardless of its source. Also called the Law of Definite Composition; confirmed by Proust's experiments on cupric carbonate. §6

---

> [!important] **Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton, 1803)** *(NEET/JEE)*
> When two elements form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other are in a ratio of small whole numbers. §6
>
> Example: H₂O and H₂O₂ — for a fixed mass of H, the combining masses of O are 16 g and 32 g, a ratio of 1:2.

---

> [!important] **Limiting Reagent (Limiting Reactant)** *(NEET/JEE — most tested)*
> The reactant **completely consumed first** in a reaction, which caps the total amount of product that can form. Found by dividing moles of each reactant by its stoichiometric coefficient — the smallest quotient identifies the limiting reagent. Comparing raw masses or mole counts directly, without dividing by coefficients, gives the wrong answer whenever the coefficients aren't equal. §12.4

---

## M

> [!note] **Mass** *(All levels)*
> The amount of matter in an object. Constant regardless of location (unlike weight), measured in kilograms (SI) or grams (lab unit: 1 kg = 1000 g), typically using an analytical balance. §4.3
>
> *Contrast with: Weight (location-dependent gravitational force on that mass — see Weight vs. Mass)*

---

> [!note] **Matter** *(All levels)*
> Anything that has **mass** and **occupies space** (has volume). By state: solid, liquid, or gas. By composition: first splits into **mixtures** (variable composition) and **pure substances** (fixed composition); pure substances then split into **elements** and **compounds**. §3.1 / §3.3

---

> [!note] **Metre (m)** *(Board)*
> The SI base unit of **length**, defined as the distance light travels in vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second. §4.2

---

> [!note] **Mixture** *(All levels)*
> Matter formed by combining **two or more pure substances** in any ratio, so its composition is variable. Its properties sit between those of its components, and it can be separated back into those components by physical methods alone. §3.3
>
> *Contrast with: Compound (fixed ratio, chemical separation only)*

---

> [!note] **Molality (m)** *(NEET/JEE)*
> Moles of solute per **kilogram of solvent**. Unit: mol kg⁻¹. **Temperature-independent**, since mass doesn't change with temperature — preferred over molarity for colligative-property calculations for exactly this reason. §13.4
>
> $$m = \frac{\text{moles of solute}}{\text{mass of solvent in kg}}$$
>
> *Contrast with: Molarity (temperature-dependent, uses volume of solution)*

---

> [!important] **Molar Mass (M)** *(All levels)*
> The mass of **one mole** of a substance, in g mol⁻¹ — numerically equal to the atomic, molecular, or formula mass of that substance in u. §9.3
>
> Example: molar mass of H₂O = 18.02 g mol⁻¹.

---

> [!important] **Molarity (M)** *(All levels)*
> Moles of solute per **litre of solution**. Unit: mol L⁻¹. **Temperature-dependent**, because the volume of a solution changes with temperature — the most commonly used concentration unit in lab chemistry despite this. §13.3
>
> $$M = \frac{\text{moles of solute}}{\text{volume of solution in litres}}$$
>
> Dilution formula (moles of solute conserved): $M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2$
>
> *Contrast with: Molality (temperature-independent, uses mass of solvent)*

---

> [!important] **Mole (mol)** *(All levels — most fundamental concept)*
> The SI base unit of **amount of substance**. One mole contains exactly $6.02214076 \times 10^{23}$ elementary entities (Avogadro's number) — which may be atoms, molecules, ions, electrons, or any other specified particle, provided the entity is stated. §9.1

---

> [!note] **Molecular Formula** *(Board/NEET)*
> The chemical formula showing the **actual number** of each type of atom in one molecule of a compound. Obtained by multiplying the empirical formula by $n = \text{Molar mass} / \text{Empirical formula mass}$. §11

---

> [!note] **Molecular Mass** *(Board/NEET)*
> The sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in one molecule, in atomic mass units (u). Used for covalent compounds that exist as discrete molecules — for ionic lattices, see Formula Mass instead. §8.3

---

> [!note] **Molecule** *(All levels)*
> The smallest particle of a substance that carries the chemical properties of that substance and can exist independently. May consist of atoms of one element (H₂, O₂) or of different elements (H₂O, CO₂). §3.3 / §8.3

---

## N

> [!note] **Nanotechnology**
> Technology dealing with structures or particles at the nanometre ($10^{-9}$ m) scale. Mentioned here because *Charaka Samhita* bhasmas — Ayurvedic metal preparations — are now known to contain nanoparticles. §1.2

---

## P

> [!note] **Paramānu**
> The ancient Indian term for the smallest indivisible particle of matter, proposed by **Acharya Kanda (600 BCE)** in the *Vaiseshika Sutras* — described as eternal, indestructible, spherical, and in motion. Comparable to the modern atom, and conceptualised roughly 2500 years before Dalton's Atomic Theory. §1.2

---

> [!note] **Philosopher's Stone (Paras)**
> A mythical substance sought by alchemists, believed to convert base metals (iron, copper) into gold. Along with the Elixir of Life, it drove early chemical investigation. §1.1

---

> [!note] **Physical Property** *(Board)*
> A property that can be measured or observed **without changing the chemical identity** of the substance. Examples: colour, density, melting point, boiling point, mass, volume. §4.1
>
> *Contrast with: Chemical Property (requires a chemical change to observe)*

---

> [!info] **Precision** *(Board/NEET)*
> The closeness of a set of repeated measurements to **each other** — reproducibility, not correctness. Does not by itself indicate closeness to the true value; a smaller least count on the instrument gives greater precision. §5.3
>
> *Contrast with: Accuracy (closeness to the true value, independent of precision)*

---

> [!note] **Pure Substance** *(All levels)*
> Matter with a **fixed composition** and definite, characteristic properties throughout — every constituent particle is chemically identical. Splits into elements and compounds. §3.1 / §3.3
>
> *Contrast with: Mixture (variable composition)*

---

## R

> [!note] **Rasayan Shastra**
> The ancient Indian name for chemistry (also *Rastantra*, *Ras Kriya*, *Rasvidya*), encompassing metallurgy, medicine, and the manufacture of cosmetics, glass, and dyes. §1.2

---

> [!note] **Rasratnakar**
> A text by Nagarjuna on the preparation of mercury compounds and the extraction of metals — gold, silver, tin, copper. §1.2

---

> [!note] **Rounding Off** *(All levels)*
> Reducing the number of digits in a calculated result to match the precision of the measurements it came from. §5.2
>
> | Digit to drop | Action |
> |:---:|:---|
> | > 5 | Raise preceding digit by 1 |
> | < 5 | Leave preceding digit unchanged |
> | = 5, preceding digit even | Leave unchanged |
> | = 5, preceding digit odd | Raise preceding digit by 1 |

---

## S

> [!note] **Scientific Notation** *(All levels)*
> Expressing a number in the form $N \times 10^n$, with $1 \leq N < 10$ — used for very large or very small numbers, and to make the number of significant figures unambiguous. §5.1

---

> [!important] **Significant Figures** *(All levels — very important)*
> All digits in a measurement known with certainty, **plus one final estimated (uncertain) digit**. Indicate how precise a measurement is; specific rules govern which zeros count (see Rounding Off for the companion rules on reporting calculated results). §5.2

---

> [!important] **SI System (Système International d'Unités)** *(All levels)*
> The internationally agreed system of measurement, established in **1960** by the **11th CGPM** (General Conference on Weights and Measures), built on **7 base units**. Replaced earlier systems such as the English and metric-but-not-SI systems. §4.2

---

> [!note] **Solute**
> The component of a solution present in the **smaller** amount — the substance being dissolved. §13
>
> *Contrast with: Solvent (the component present in larger amount, doing the dissolving)*

---

> [!note] **Solution**
> A **homogeneous mixture** of two or more substances — a solute dissolved in a solvent — with uniform composition throughout. §3.3 / §13

---

> [!note] **Solvent**
> The component of a solution present in the **larger** amount — the substance that does the dissolving. Water is the most common solvent, but not the only one. §13
>
> *Contrast with: Solute (the component present in smaller amount, being dissolved)*

---

> [!note] **Stoichiometric Coefficient** *(Board/NEET)*
> The numerical coefficient placed before a chemical formula in a balanced equation. Represents the number of **molecules or moles** of that species participating in the reaction — never change a subscript to balance an equation, only these coefficients. §12.2

---

> [!important] **Stoichiometry** *(NEET/JEE)*
> *(Greek: stoicheion = element, metron = measure)* — the quantitative study of the amounts of reactants consumed and products formed in a chemical reaction, based on a balanced chemical equation. §12.1

---

> [!warning] **STP / NTP (Molar Volume of a Gas)** *(NEET/JEE — genuine source conflict, flag before using)*
> The volume occupied by **one mole of any gas** at standard conditions — but the reference pressure itself has two conventions in circulation, so the constant to use depends on which one a problem intends:
>
> | Convention | Conditions | Molar volume |
> |:---|:---:|:---:|
> | Old STP, commonly labelled NTP | 0 °C, 1 atm | **22.4 L mol⁻¹** |
> | Current IUPAC STP | 0 °C, 1 bar | **22.7 L mol⁻¹** |
>
> **For this chapter:** default to **22.7 L mol⁻¹** (current NCERT), but use **22.4 L mol⁻¹** whenever a problem explicitly says NTP or is sourced from older material — state which one you used. §9.2

---

## U

> [!note] **Unified Mass Unit (u)** *(All levels)*
> The modern preferred symbol for the atomic mass unit (amu), defined as 1/12 the mass of one ¹²C atom. §8.1
>
> $$1 \text{ u} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24} \text{ g}$$

---

> [!note] **Unit Factor**
> A ratio equal to **1**, used in dimensional analysis to convert between units — numerator and denominator represent the same quantity in different units. §5.4
>
> Example: $\dfrac{2.54 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ in}} = 1$

---

## V

> [!note] **Vaiseshika Sutras**
> The text authored by **Acharya Kanda (600 BCE)** containing the first formulation of atomic theory (the Paramānu concept) in India. §1.2

---

## W

> [!warning] **Weight vs. Mass** *(Board/NEET)*
> Weight is the gravitational force on an object's mass: $W = mg$. §4.3
>
> | Property | Weight | Mass |
> |:---:|:---:|:---:|
> | SI unit | Newton (N) | kilogram (kg) |
> | Location-dependent? | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
> | Measured by | Spring balance | Analytical balance |
