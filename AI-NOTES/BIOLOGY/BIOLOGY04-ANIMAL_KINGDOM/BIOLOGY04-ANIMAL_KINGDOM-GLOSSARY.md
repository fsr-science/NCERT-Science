# Biology | Chapter 04 | Animal Kingdom | GLOSSARY

> Every term below is isolated in a standardised callout box, with Greek/Latin roots dissected explicitly to build intuitive, lasting recognition of classification vocabulary.

---

> [!info] Coelom (Greek: *koiloma* = cavity) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A body cavity that is completely lined by mesoderm on both the body-wall side and the gut side, present in all "higher" triploblastic phyla from Annelida onward.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Present in Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, and Chordata; used to house and cushion internal organs.

---

> [!info] Pseudocoelom (Greek: *pseudo* = false + *koiloma* = cavity) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A body cavity present between the mesoderm and endoderm, arising from the persistence of the blastocoel, and therefore **not** lined by mesoderm on both surfaces.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Characteristic feature exclusively of phylum Aschelminthes (e.g., *Ascaris*).

---

> [!info] Metamerism (Greek: *meta* = after + *meros* = part) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The condition in which the body is externally and internally divided into a series of repeating segments, with at least some organs repeated within each segment.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Diagnostic feature of phylum Annelida; visible as external body rings in the earthworm (*Pheretima*).

---

> [!info] Notochord (Greek: *noton* = back + Latin *chorda* = cord) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A mesodermally-derived, dorsal, rod-shaped, supportive structure that runs the length of the body, separating the nerve cord from the gut during development.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Present at some life stage in all Chordata; persists throughout life in Cephalochordata and Chondrichthyes, but is replaced by a vertebral column in adult Vertebrata.

---

> [!info] Spongocoel (Latin: *spongia* = sponge + Greek *koilos* = hollow) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** The large central cavity of a sponge's body into which water, drawn in via ostia, flows before exiting through the osculum.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in phylum Porifera; lined internally by flagellated choanocytes that maintain water current.

---

> [!info] Choanocytes / Collar Cells (Greek: *choane* = funnel) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** Flagellated cells bearing a collar of microvilli that line the spongocoel and internal canals, generating water currents for feeding, respiration, and waste removal.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Exclusive to phylum Porifera; project into the spongocoel and radial canals.

---

> [!info] Cnidoblasts / Cnidocytes (Greek: *knide* = nettle) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Specialised stinging cells present on the tentacles and body of cnidarians, used for anchorage, defence, and capture of prey.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Diagnostic cell type of phylum Coelenterata (Cnidaria); densest on the tentacles surrounding the mouth.

---

> [!info] Polymorphism (Greek: *poly* = many + *morphe* = form) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** The occurrence of two or more structurally and functionally distinct individual forms (e.g., polyp and medusa) within the same species.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Exhibited by certain coelenterates such as *Obelia*, which alternates between a sessile polyp and a free-swimming medusa stage.

---

> [!info] Flame Cells (descriptive term, from flickering ciliary tuft appearance) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** Specialised excretory/osmoregulatory cells bearing a tuft of cilia that beats in a flame-like manner, helping remove excess fluid and waste.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in phylum Platyhelminthes (e.g., *Planaria*), distributed along the excretory canals.

---

> [!info] Regeneration (Latin: *re* = again + *generare* = to produce) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** The biological capacity of an organism to regrow lost or damaged body parts, sometimes to the extent of forming an entirely new individual from a fragment.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Remarkably developed in *Planaria* (Platyhelminthes) and in sponges (Porifera, via fragmentation).

---

> [!info] Haemocoel (Greek: *haima* = blood + *koilos* = hollow) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A blood-filled body cavity characteristic of animals with an open circulatory system, in which blood bathes tissues directly rather than remaining confined to vessels.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Present in Arthropoda and most Mollusca (except cephalopods), replacing the role of a true coelom in circulation.

---

> [!info] Malpighian Tubules (named after anatomist Marcello Malpighi) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Thin, blind excretory tubules that remove nitrogenous waste from the haemolymph and empty it into the hindgut for elimination.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in most terrestrial arthropods (e.g., insects), opening into the junction of the midgut and hindgut.

---

> [!info] Mantle (Old English: *mentel* = cloak) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** A soft, spongy sheet of tissue that covers the visceral hump of molluscs and is responsible for secreting the calcareous shell.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Diagnostic structure of phylum Mollusca; encloses the mantle cavity, which houses the ctenidia (gills).

---

> [!info] Radula (Latin: *radere* = to scrape) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** A rasping, file-like feeding structure bearing rows of chitinous teeth, used to scrape food particles from surfaces.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Present in the mouth cavity of most molluscs (e.g., *Pila*), but notably **absent in bivalves**.

---

> [!info] Water Vascular System (descriptive term) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A hydraulically-operated network of water-filled canals unique to echinoderms, used for locomotion, food capture and transport, and respiration.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Diagnostic system of phylum Echinodermata (e.g., *Asterias*), operating the characteristic tube feet.

---

> [!info] Stomochord (Greek: *stoma* = mouth + *chorde* = cord) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A short, tubular outgrowth in the collar region that superficially resembles a true notochord but is not homologous to it.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in the collar region of Hemichordata (e.g., *Balanoglossus*); historically caused Hemichordata to be misclassified as chordates.

---

> [!info] Operculum (Latin: *operire* = to cover) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A protective bony flap covering and protecting the gill slits, allowing controlled, efficient unidirectional water flow over the gills.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Present in Class Osteichthyes (bony fish); characteristically **absent** in Class Chondrichthyes.

---

> [!info] Placoid Scales (Greek: *plax* = flat plate) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** Small, tooth-like dermal scales composed of dentine and enamel-like material, covering the skin of cartilaginous fishes.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in Class Chondrichthyes; also modified to form the teeth of sharks and rays.

---

> [!info] Metamorphosis (Greek: *meta* = change + *morphe* = form) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A biological process involving abrupt physical transformation from a larval form to a structurally distinct adult form.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Characteristic of Class Amphibia (e.g., tadpole → adult frog in *Rana*).

---

> [!info] Thecodont Dentition (Greek: *theke* = case/socket + *odont* = tooth) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A dental arrangement in which teeth are embedded in individual sockets within the jawbone, allowing firm anchorage.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Characteristic of Class Mammalia, alongside heterodont (differentiated) tooth types.

---

> [!info] Homeothermous (Greek: *homoios* = similar + *therme* = heat) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** The physiological capacity to maintain a constant internal body temperature independent of the external environment; commonly termed "warm-blooded."
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** A defining physiological feature of Class Aves and Class Mammalia, contrasted with the poikilothermous ("cold-blooded") reptiles, amphibians, and fish.

---

> [!info] Viviparity (Latin: *vivus* = alive + *parere* = to bring forth) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A mode of reproduction in which the developing embryo receives nourishment directly from the mother and is born as a fully-formed young individual rather than hatching from an externally laid egg.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** The dominant reproductive mode in Class Mammalia (with the notable exception of egg-laying prototherians such as *Ornithorhynchus*).

---

> [!info] Ossicles (Latin: *ossiculum* = small bone) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** Small, calcareous, plate-like skeletal elements embedded in the body wall, collectively forming a spiny internal skeleton.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Present in phylum Echinodermata, giving the "spiny-skinned" texture that names the phylum.

---

> [!info] Pneumatic Bones (Greek: *pneuma* = air) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** Fully-ossified, lightweight bones that contain internal air cavities, reducing overall body weight without compromising structural strength.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Characteristic skeletal adaptation of Class Aves, essential for flight.
