
# 📖 BIOLOGY XI — PLANT KINGDOM

### *Terminology & Etymology Vault | Dictionary-Style Reference*

---

> [!info] Thallus (Greek: *thallos* = a young shoot/sprout) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A simple plant body that is not differentiated into true root, stem, and leaves, characteristic of algae and many bryophytes.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** The entire vegetative body of algae and thalloid liverworts such as *Marchantia*.

---

> [!info] Cryptogams (Greek: *kryptos* = hidden + *gamos* = marriage/reproductive organ) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Plants whose reproductive organs are inconspicuous or "hidden," and which do not produce seeds — comprising Algae, Bryophytes, and Pteridophytes.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Encompasses the entire non-seed-bearing division of Kingdom Plantae.

---

> [!info] Phanerogams (Greek: *phaneros* = visible + *gamos* = reproductive organ) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Seed-bearing plants with well-differentiated, visible reproductive organs — comprising Gymnosperms and Angiosperms.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Encompasses all seed-producing members of Kingdom Plantae.

---

> [!info] Isogamy (Greek: *isos* = equal + *gamos* = marriage) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A form of sexual reproduction in algae involving the fusion of two gametes that are morphologically similar (may be motile/flagellated or non-motile).
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Observed in simpler algal genera such as certain *Chlamydomonas* species.

---

> [!info] Anisogamy (Greek: *anisos* = unequal + *gamos* = marriage) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** Fusion between two motile gametes of dissimilar size, representing an intermediate stage of gamete differentiation in algae.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in certain species of *Chlamydomonas* and other algae showing intermediate gamete specialisation.

---

> [!info] Oogamy (Greek: *oon* = egg + *gamos* = marriage) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The most advanced type of sexual reproduction, involving fusion between one large, non-motile female gamete (egg) and one small, motile male gamete.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in *Volvox*, *Fucus*, and all higher plant groups (bryophytes onward).

---

> [!info] Holdfast (descriptive compound term) — [Exam Target: Board] — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A root-like anchoring structure found at the base of brown algae, attaching the thallus firmly to the substratum.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Basal region of the algal body in Phaeophyceae members such as *Laminaria* and *Sargassum*.

---

> [!info] Rhizoid (Greek: *rhiza* = root + *-oid* = resembling) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A root-like filamentous structure (unicellular or multicellular) that anchors the gametophyte body to the substratum and aids in absorption, but lacks the internal complexity of a true root.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Base of the thallus in bryophytes (mosses, liverworts).

---

> [!info] Antheridium (plural: Antheridia) (Greek: *antheros* = flowery + *-idion* = small) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The male sex organ of bryophytes and pteridophytes, producing biflagellate, motile male gametes called antherozoids.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Borne on the gametophyte (prothallus) of bryophytes and pteridophytes.

---

> [!info] Archegonium (plural: Archegonia) (Greek: *arche* = beginning + *gonos* = offspring) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The flask-shaped female sex organ of bryophytes and pteridophytes, producing a single non-motile egg cell.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Borne on the gametophyte of bryophytes and pteridophytes.

---

> [!info] Protonema (Greek: *protos* = first + *nema* = thread) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** The first, filamentous, chloronemal growth stage that develops directly from a germinating moss spore, prior to the development of the leafy gametophyte.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Initial developmental stage of moss gametophytes, e.g., *Funaria*.

---

> [!info] Gemma (plural: Gemmae) (Latin: *gemma* = bud) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A small, multicellular, asexual reproductive structure produced in specialised cup-like receptacles, which detaches and develops into a new plant.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Formed within gemma cups on the thallus surface of liverworts such as *Marchantia*.

---

> [!info] Homosporous (Greek: *homos* = same + Latin *spora* = seed) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Describing pteridophytes that produce only one type of spore, morphologically and functionally similar, giving rise to bisexual gametophytes.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** *Pteris* and *Lycopodium* sporangia.

---

> [!info] Heterosporous (Greek: *heteros* = different + Latin *spora* = seed) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Describing pteridophytes (and all seed plants) that produce two morphologically distinct spore types — microspores (male) and megaspores (female).
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** *Selaginella* and *Salvinia*; also universally in gymnosperms and angiosperms.

---

> [!info] Microspore (Greek: *mikros* = small + Latin *spora* = seed) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The smaller of the two spore types in heterosporous plants, which germinates to form the male gametophyte.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Produced in microsporangia of heterosporous pteridophytes, gymnosperms, and angiosperms.

---

> [!info] Megaspore (Greek: *megas* = large + Latin *spora* = seed) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The larger of the two spore types in heterosporous plants, which germinates to form the female gametophyte.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Produced in megasporangia of heterosporous pteridophytes, gymnosperms, and angiosperms.

---

> [!info] Coralloid Roots (Latin: *corallium* = coral + *-oid* = resembling) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** Small, specialised, coral-shaped roots associated symbiotically with nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in the root system of gymnosperms such as *Cycas*.

---

> [!info] Strobilus (plural: Strobili) (Greek: *strobilos* = pine cone) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A cone-like reproductive structure in gymnosperms, comprising an aggregation of sporophylls bearing sporangia.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Male and female cones of *Pinus* and *Cycas*.

---

> [!info] Double Fertilisation (descriptive compound term) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A reproductive phenomenon exclusive to angiosperms in which one male gamete fuses with the egg to form the zygote, while a second male gamete fuses with the polar nuclei to form the triploid primary endosperm nucleus.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Occurs within the embryo sac of the ovule in flowering plants.

---

> [!info] Gametophyte (Greek: *gametes* = spouse + *phyton* = plant) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The haploid, gamete-producing generation in the alternation of generations of plant life cycles.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Dominant in bryophytes; a reduced, dependent structure within the sporophyte in gymnosperms and angiosperms.

---

> [!info] Sporophyte (Greek: *spora* = seed + *phyton* = plant) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The diploid, spore-producing generation in the alternation of generations of plant life cycles, arising from the fertilised zygote.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Dominant and independent in pteridophytes, gymnosperms, and angiosperms; dependent on the gametophyte in bryophytes.

---

> [!info] Agar (Malay: *agar-agar*, from red algae) — [Exam Target: Board]
> **Scientific Definition:** A gelatinous hydrocolloid extracted from red algae, widely used as a solidifying agent for microbiological culture media.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Extracted commercially from red algal genera *Gelidium* and *Gracilaria*.
