### Section A

- **B1.** Cryptogams: plants with "hidden" reproductive organs, not seed-bearing (e.g., Algae/Bryophytes/Pteridophytes). Phanerogams: plants with visible reproductive organs, seed-bearing (e.g., Gymnosperms/Angiosperms).
- **B2.** Oogamy is the fusion between one large, non-motile female gamete (egg) and one small, motile male gamete. Example: *Volvox* or *Fucus*.
- **B3.** Because bryophytes can live in soil but are dependent on external water for the transfer of motile male gametes to the archegonium during fertilisation, similar to amphibians needing water for reproduction while living partly on land.
- **B4.** Heterosporous plants produce two morphologically distinct spore types — microspores and megaspores. Example: *Selaginella*.
- **B5.** In gymnosperms, ovules are not enclosed by any ovary wall; after fertilisation, seeds remain exposed/uncovered since no fruit forms around them.

### Section B

- **B6.** Chlorophyceae: chlorophyll a & b dominant, stores starch. Phaeophyceae: fucoxanthin dominant, stores mannitol and laminarin. Rhodophyceae: r-phycoerythrin dominant, stores floridean starch.
- **B7.** The gametophyte is the dominant, photosynthetic, independent phase bearing antheridia and archegonia. After fertilisation, the resulting sporophyte remains permanently attached to and nutritionally dependent on the parent gametophyte — it is never free-living.
- **B8.** In heterosporous pteridophytes, the megaspore and microspore are retained on the parent sporophyte and germinate there to form female and male gametophytes respectively — this retention of the megasporangium on the sporophyte closely parallels the ovule structure of seed plants, making heterospory the evolutionary precursor to the true seed habit seen in gymnosperms and angiosperms.
- **B9.** (1) Agar (from *Gelidium*/*Gracilaria*) used in microbial culture media. (2) Algin (from brown algae) used as a hydrocolloid/thickener. (3) *Laminaria* and *Sargassum* used directly as food; carrageen (from red algae) used commercially as well.
- **B10.** (i) Dicots have 2 cotyledons; monocots have 1. (ii) Dicots show reticulate leaf venation; monocots show parallel venation. (iii) Dicots typically have tap roots; monocots typically have fibrous roots.

### Section C

- **B11.** (a) Haplontic life cycle. (b) Zygotic meiosis; example — *Spirogyra* or *Volvox*.
- **B12.** (a) Holdfast (base) → Stipe (stalk) → Frond (leaf-like blade). (b) *Laminaria*, *Sargassum*, or *Fucus*.
- **B13.** Plant group: Pteridophytes. X = Germination into a gametophyte (prothallus); Y = Fertilisation.

### Section D

- **N1.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains why gymnosperm gametophytes lack independent existence.
- **N2.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains the function of coralloid roots.
- **N3.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains the moisture dependency causing bryophyte habitat restriction.
- **N4.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains why angiosperms are considered more evolutionarily advanced.

### Section E

- **N5.** (a) — (i) and (ii) are correct. (iii) is incorrect — Rhodophyceae are predominantly marine, not fresh water. (iv) is incorrect — algin is extracted from brown algae (Phaeophyceae), not red algae.
- **N6.** (c) — (i) is incorrect (the main plant body of a pteridophyte is the sporophyte, not the gametophyte); (iii) is actually a correct statement, not incorrect — re-evaluate: only (i) and (iv) are incorrect, since (iii) correctly describes heterospory and *Selaginella* is heterosporous (making iv incorrect for calling it homosporous). Correct final selection: **(a) (i) and (iv) only**.
- **N7.** (a) — A-2 (Chlorophyceae: Starch), B-3 (Phaeophyceae: Mannitol/Laminarin), C-1 (Rhodophyceae: Floridean starch).
- **N8.** (a) — A-4 (Haplontic: *Spirogyra*), B-3 (Diplontic: Angiosperms), C-1 (Haplodiplontic gametophyte-dominant: Bryophytes), D-2 (Haplodiplontic sporophyte-dominant: Pteridophytes).

### Section F/G

- **N9.** (a) The sporophyte producing haploid (n=12) microspores via meiosis must itself be diploid, so the sporophyte's chromosome number is **2n = 24**. (b) The male gametophyte develops directly from the haploid microspore without any further reduction, so it retains **n = 12** chromosomes.
- **N10.** (a) The megaspore is produced by meiosis from the diploid sporophyte (2n = 24), so it is haploid: **n = 12**. (b) The primary endosperm nucleus forms from the fusion of one haploid male gamete (n = 12) with two haploid polar nuclei (n = 12 each), giving a total of **3n = 36** chromosomes (triploid).
