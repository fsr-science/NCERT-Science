For each question, choose the correct option:
&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) A is true, but R is false.
&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) A is false, but R is true.

**N1. Assertion (A):** *Panthera leo* and *Panthera tigris* belong to the same genus but different species.
**Reason (R):** Species sharing a genus exhibit fewer similarities with each other than species placed in different genera.

**N2. Assertion (A):** Systematics is considered a broader field than Taxonomy.
**Reason (R):** Systematics explicitly incorporates the evolutionary relationships between organisms, in addition to identification, nomenclature, and classification.

**N3. Assertion (A):** The taxonomic rank "Division" is applied to the classification of the housefly.
**Reason (R):** "Division" and "Phylum" are interchangeable terms applicable uniformly across the Plant and Animal Kingdoms.

**N4. Assertion (A):** Mango and Wheat, though both classified under division Angiospermae, are placed in different classes.
**Reason (R):** Angiosperms are divided into Dicotyledonae and Monocotyledonae at the class level based on cotyledon number.

**N10.** A taxonomist describes a newly discovered flowering plant and assigns it the name "*Rosa indica* Linn." Identify what each of the three components of this citation represents.

### Section A

- **B1.** A taxon is a unit/category of classification at any rank. Kingdom-level example: *Animalia*. Species-level example: *sapiens* (in *Homo sapiens*).
- **B2.** Biodiversity is the total variety and number of living organisms on Earth. Approximately **1.7 to 1.8 million** species have been described so far.
- **B3.** Nomenclature is the process of assigning a universally accepted scientific name to an identified organism. It is necessary because local/common names vary from region to region, causing confusion; a single standardised name allows unambiguous global communication among scientists.
- **B4.** Identification = correctly describing an organism to know exactly which organism is being referred to. Classification = grouping identified organisms into convenient categories based on shared characteristics.
- **B5.** The taxonomic hierarchy is the ranked series of taxonomic categories (Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum/Division → Kingdom) arranged from lowest to highest, through which any organism can be systematically classified.

### Section B

- **B6.** (1) Biological names are generally Latin and written in italics. (2) The first word denotes the genus, the second the specific epithet. (3) Both words are underlined separately when handwritten. (4) The genus name starts with a capital letter, the specific epithet with a small letter.
- **B7.** (i) Taxonomy covers characterisation, identification, classification, and nomenclature; Systematics covers all of this **plus** evolutionary relationships. (ii) Systematics is broader in scope and was historically enlarged beyond taxonomy's narrower definition.
- **B8.** Phylum is used only for the Animal Kingdom while Division is used only for the Plant Kingdom — e.g., animals sharing a notochord are placed in phylum Chordata, while plants like mango and wheat sharing seed-bearing flowering features are placed in division Angiospermae. Using "Phylum" for a plant or "Division" for an animal is taxonomically incorrect.
- **B9.** Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.
- **B10.** Errors: (1) The genus name "mangifera" should begin with a capital letter → "*Mangifera*". (2) The specific epithet "Indica" should begin with a small letter → "indica". Correct name: ***Mangifera indica***.

### Section C

- **B11.** (a) A = Kingdom (highest, widest — most organisms, fewest shared traits); G = Species (lowest, narrowest — fewest organisms, most shared traits). (b) The band representing Class (since lion belongs to class Mammalia). (c) Decreases from A to G is incorrect phrasing to watch for — shared characteristics actually **increase** as you move from A (Kingdom) down to G (Species), i.e., they decrease as you move upward from Species to Kingdom.
- **B12.** Housefly | *Musca domestica* | *Musca* | Muscidae | Diptera | Insecta | Arthropoda.
- **B13.** X = Identification; Y = Nomenclature.

### Section D

- **N1.** (b) — Both true, but R is not the correct explanation: species within the *same* genus (like *Panthera leo* and *Panthera tigris*) actually share **more** characteristics with each other than with species of other genera; R as stated describes the wrong relationship direction for explaining A.
- **N2.** (a) — Both true and R correctly explains why Systematics is broader.
- **N3.** (d) — A is false (housefly, an animal, is classified using Phylum — Arthropoda — not Division); R is false too actually, since Phylum and Division are explicitly NOT interchangeable (Phylum = animals only, Division = plants only). Correct evaluation: A is false, and R is also false as an absolute statement — flag this as a "spot the double error" style trap; the intended answer per strict NCERT logic is that neither statement holds, reinforcing that Phylum and Division must never be interchanged.
- **N4.** (a) — Both true, and R correctly explains why mango and wheat diverge at the class level despite shared division.

### Section E

- **N5.** (a) — (i), (iii), (iv) correct. (ii) is incorrect because only the genus name is capitalised; the specific epithet always begins with a lowercase letter.
- **N6.** (c) — (i) is incorrect (ICZN governs animals, not plants — ICBN governs plants); (ii) is a correct statement, not incorrect (a genus can indeed hold one or many species) — re-check: only (i) and (iii) are actually incorrect since family in plants uses **both** vegetative and reproductive features, not reproductive alone. Correct selection: **(a) (i) and (iii) only**.
- **N7.** (a) — A-2 (Order: Carnivora), B-3 (Class: Mammalia), C-1 (Phylum: Chordata), D-4 (Family: Felidae).
- **N8.** (a) — A-3 (Potato: *Solanum*), B-1 (Lion: *Panthera*), C-4 (Wheat: *Triticum*), D-2 (Man: *Homo*).

### Section F/G

- **N9.** This is a conceptual estimation exercise, not an NCERT-derived numeric fact — the intended learning point is simply that similarity score should show a **clear declining trend** moving from Species toward Kingdom; any answer demonstrating this directional understanding (e.g., roughly 40–55 units by the Class level, three steps up) is acceptable for reasoning purposes.
- **N10.** "*Rosa*" = the genus name; "*indica*" = the specific epithet; "Linn." = the abbreviated author citation, indicating that Carolus Linnaeus was the taxonomist who first formally described this species.
