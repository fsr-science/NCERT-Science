### Section D — Assertion–Reasoning Matrix

Choose: (a) Both A and R true, R explains A. (b) Both true, R does not explain A. (c) A true, R false. (d) A false, R true.

**N1. Assertion (A):** *Euglena* is classified under Kingdom Protista rather than Kingdom Plantae or Animalia.
**Reason (R):** *Euglena* shows autotrophic nutrition in sunlight and heterotrophic nutrition in the absence of light, making it difficult to place under a single kingdom.

**N2. Assertion (A):** Viruses are considered to lie at the boundary between living and non-living entities.
**Reason (R):** Viruses can be crystallised like inert chemical substances but replicate and show metabolic activity only inside a living host cell.

**N3. Assertion (A):** Mycoplasma are classified under Eubacteria despite lacking a cell wall.
**Reason (R):** All Eubacteria, by definition, must possess a peptidoglycan cell wall.

**N4. Assertion (A):** Slime moulds are grouped under Kingdom Protista and not Kingdom Fungi.
**Reason (R):** Slime moulds produce a plasmodial, motile, naked mass of protoplasm during their vegetative phase, unlike the walled hyphal structure of true fungi.

### Section A

- **B1.** R.H. Whittaker, in **1969**.
- **B2.** Mycelium = the network of hyphae forming the fungal body. Hyphae = long, slender, thread-like filaments (septate or aseptate) that collectively make up the mycelium.
- **B3.** A heterocyst is a specialised, thick-walled cell in certain filamentous cyanobacteria that serves as the site of nitrogen fixation; found in *Nostoc*.
- **B4.** A lichen is a symbiotic association between an alga (phycobiont, autotrophic) and a fungus (mycobiont, heterotrophic), functioning as a single composite organism.
- **B5.** Viroids are infectious agents smaller than viruses, consisting only of free, low-molecular-weight RNA with **no protein coat**, unlike viruses which possess a protein capsid around their nucleic acid.

### Section B

- **B6.** Cell structure, body organisation (thallus organisation), mode of nutrition, mode of reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships.
- **B7.** (1) Fungi have a cell wall made of chitin, not cellulose like true plants. (2) Fungi are heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic), lacking chlorophyll and the capacity for photosynthesis, unlike autotrophic plants.
- **B8.** (i) Archaebacteria have a distinct cell wall enabling extreme-habitat survival; Eubacteria have a peptidoglycan wall. (ii) Archaebacteria include halophiles/thermoacidophiles/methanogens; Eubacteria include cyanobacteria/mycoplasma. (iii) Archaebacteria occupy extreme habitats; Eubacteria are ubiquitous.
- **B9.** (1) Plasmogamy — fusion of protoplasms of two gametes. (2) Karyogamy — fusion of the two nuclei (may be delayed, producing a dikaryotic n+n phase). (3) Meiosis in the resulting diploid zygote, producing haploid spores.
- **B10.** They are called "Fungi Imperfecti" because only their asexual/vegetative reproductive phases have been observed — no sexual stage is known. If a sexual stage is later discovered in a species, it is immediately reclassified into either Ascomycetes or Basidiomycetes, since the Deuteromycetes classification is provisional.

### Section C

- **B11.** (a) Septate hypha (cross-walls present). (b) Phycomycetes possess aseptate/coenocytic hyphae — continuous tubes with multinucleate cytoplasm and no cross-walls.
- **B12.** (a) Dinoflagellates. (b) "Red tide" — caused by rapid multiplication of red-pigmented dinoflagellates such as *Gonyaulax*, releasing toxins that can kill marine fish.
- **B13.** X = Plasmogamy; Y = Karyogamy; Z = Meiosis.

### Section D

- **N1.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains why *Euglena*'s dual nutritional mode places it at a kingdom boundary, justifying its placement in Protista.
- **N2.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains the living/non-living boundary nature of viruses.
- **N3.** (c) — A is true (Mycoplasma are indeed classified under Eubacteria); R is false, since Mycoplasma is the well-known **exception** — not all Eubacteria possess a cell wall.
- **N4.** (a) — Both true, R correctly explains why the plasmodial, wall-less vegetative phase of slime moulds places them in Protista rather than Fungi.

### Section E

- **N5.** (a) — (i), (iii), (iv) are correct. (ii) is incorrect because cyanobacteria are photosynthetic (autotrophic), not heterotrophic.
- **N6.** (a) — (i) is incorrect (fungal cell wall is chitin, not cellulose); (iv) is incorrect (only Phycomycetes are aseptate/coenocytic; Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, and Deuteromycetes have septate hyphae). (ii) and (iii) are both correct statements.
- **N7.** (a) — A-3 (Phycomycetes: Zygospores), B-4 (Ascomycetes: Ascospores), C-1 (Basidiomycetes: Basidiospores), D-2 (Deuteromycetes: None known).
- **N8.** (a) — A-3 (Ivanowsky: recognised tobacco mosaic contagion, 1892), B-4 (Beijerinck: "contagium vivum fluidum"), C-1 (Stanley: crystallised viruses), D-2 (Diener: discovered viroids).

### Section F/G

- **N9.** 2 hours = 120 minutes = 6 generation intervals of 20 minutes each. Starting from 1 cell: $1 \times 2^6 = 64$ cells. **Answer: 64 bacterial cells.**
- **N10.** (a) During the dikaryophase, each nucleus individually remains **haploid**, so each of the two nuclei present per cell carries **n = 8** chromosomes (the cell as a whole is described as n+n, i.e., 8+8, but each individual nucleus is still haploid at 8). (b) Immediately after karyogamy, the two haploid nuclei fuse to form a single diploid zygote nucleus with **2n = 16** chromosomes. (c) After meiosis, the diploid zygote (2n = 16) divides to produce haploid spores, each again carrying **n = 8** chromosomes.
