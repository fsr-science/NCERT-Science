
# 📖 BIOLOGY XI — BIOLOGICAL CLASSIFICATION

### *Terminology & Etymology Vault | Dictionary-Style Reference*

---

> [!info] Monera (Greek: *moneres* = single, solitary) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The kingdom comprising all prokaryotic organisms (bacteria), lacking a membrane-bound nucleus and true cell organelles.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Ubiquitous — soil, water, air, extreme environments (hot springs, salt lakes), and inside living tissues of plants, animals, and humans.

---

> [!info] Protista (Greek: *protos* = first, primitive) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The kingdom of unicellular eukaryotic organisms showing evolutionary affinities with plants, fungi, and animals; considered the boundary/link kingdom of the five-kingdom system.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Fresh water, marine environments, moist soil, and as parasites within host organisms.

---

> [!info] Archaebacteria (Greek: *archaios* = ancient + bacterion = small rod) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A group of prokaryotes with a distinct cell wall structure enabling survival in extreme habitats such as high salinity, high temperature, and marshy anaerobic conditions.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Salt lakes (halophiles), hot springs (thermoacidophiles), marshy areas and the gut of ruminant animals (methanogens).

---

> [!info] Eubacteria (Greek: *eu* = true + bacterion = small rod) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** "True bacteria" characterised by a rigid, peptidoglycan-based cell wall and, in motile forms, a flagellum for locomotion.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Nearly all habitats on Earth — soil, water, air, and as commensals/pathogens within host organisms.

---

> [!info] Cyanobacteria (Greek: *kyanos* = blue + bacterion = small rod) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Photosynthetic, autotrophic prokaryotes (blue-green algae) possessing chlorophyll a; some filamentous forms bear heterocysts specialised for nitrogen fixation.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Fresh water, marine, and terrestrial environments; e.g., colonial genus *Nostoc*.

---

> [!info] Heterocyst (Greek: *heteros* = different + *kystis* = bladder/cell) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A specialised, thick-walled cell found in certain filamentous cyanobacteria that serves as the site of biological nitrogen fixation.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Interspersed along the filaments of nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria such as *Nostoc* and *Anabaena*.

---

> [!info] Mycoplasma (Greek: *mykes* = fungus + *plasma* = formed/moulded) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** The only group of bacteria that completely lack a cell wall, making them the smallest known living cells capable of independent existence.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found as pathogens in plants and animals; can survive without oxygen.

---

> [!info] Chrysophytes (Greek: *chrysos* = golden + *phyton* = plant) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A protistan group including diatoms and golden algae (desmids), characterised by a silica-embedded, indestructible two-part overlapping cell wall.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Float as passive plankton in fresh water and marine habitats; fossilised remains form "diatomaceous earth."

---

> [!info] Dinoflagellates (Greek: *dinos* = whirling + Latin *flagellum* = whip) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Mostly marine, photosynthetic protists with stiff cellulose cell-wall plates and two flagella (one longitudinal, one transverse) lying in grooves.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Marine waters; rapid multiplication of red-pigmented species (e.g., *Gonyaulax*) causes toxic "red tides."

---

> [!info] Euglenoids (from genus *Euglena*, Greek: *eu* = true + *glene* = eyeball) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Fresh water protists lacking a true cell wall but possessing a flexible protein-rich pellicle, exhibiting both autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition depending on light availability.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Stagnant fresh water bodies; represented by the genus *Euglena*.

---

> [!info] Pellicle (Latin: *pellis* = skin) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A flexible, protein-rich outer covering (in place of a rigid cell wall) that allows body-shape flexibility in euglenoids.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Outer boundary of the euglenoid cell body, beneath the plasma membrane.

---

> [!info] Slime Moulds (descriptive term) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Saprophytic protists that form a mobile, multinucleate mass of protoplasm called a plasmodium under favourable conditions, and produce spore-bearing fruiting bodies under adverse conditions.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Decaying twigs, leaves, and organic debris in moist environments.

---

> [!info] Plasmodium (as used for slime moulds) (Greek: *plasma* = formed + *-oid* = like) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** In slime moulds, a naked, multinucleate mass of protoplasm formed by the aggregation of amoeboid cells, capable of engulfing organic matter as it moves.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Formed on decaying organic matter under favourable moist conditions.

---

> [!info] Protozoans (Greek: *protos* = first + *zoon* = animal) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Heterotrophic, predatory or parasitic unicellular protists considered evolutionarily close to animals, subdivided by locomotory organelle into amoeboid, flagellated, ciliated, and sporozoan groups.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Fresh water, marine, moist soil; several as endoparasites (e.g., gut, blood) of humans and animals.

---

> [!info] Pseudopodia (Greek: *pseudes* = false + *podos* = foot) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Temporary, finger-like cytoplasmic projections used for locomotion and capturing food by amoeboid organisms.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Formed at the cell surface of *Amoeba* and *Entamoeba* in fresh water, marine water, moist soil, or host tissue.

---

> [!info] Mycelium (Greek: *mykes* = fungus) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The collective network of thread-like fungal filaments (hyphae) that constitutes the vegetative body of a fungus.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Embedded within the substratum (soil, decaying matter, host tissue) on which the fungus grows.

---

> [!info] Hyphae (Greek: *hyphe* = web) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** Long, slender, thread-like structures that make up the fungal mycelium; may be septate (cross-walled) or aseptate/coenocytic (continuous, multinucleate).
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Constitute the entire vegetative structure of all fungi except unicellular yeasts.

---

> [!info] Chitin (Greek: *chiton* = tunic/coat) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A tough, complex nitrogenous polysaccharide that forms the rigid cell wall of fungi (distinct from the cellulose wall of plants).
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Cell wall of all members of Kingdom Fungi; also found in arthropod exoskeletons.

---

> [!info] Plasmogamy (Greek: *plasma* = formed + *gamos* = marriage) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** The first stage of fungal sexual reproduction, involving the fusion of protoplasms between two motile or non-motile gametes, without immediate nuclear fusion.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Occurs within specialised sexual reproductive structures of Ascomycetes and Basidiomycetes.

---

> [!info] Karyogamy (Greek: *karyon* = nucleus/kernel + *gamos* = marriage) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** The fusion of two haploid nuclei following plasmogamy in the fungal sexual cycle, producing a diploid zygote nucleus.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Occurs after plasmogamy, sometimes following a delayed dikaryotic phase, inside the fungal reproductive structure.

---

> [!info] Dikaryophase (Greek: *di* = two + *karyon* = nucleus) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A unique fungal life-cycle stage in which each cell contains two genetically distinct haploid nuclei (n + n) following delayed karyogamy after plasmogamy.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Found in the hyphae of Ascomycetes and Basidiomycetes between plasmogamy and eventual karyogamy.

---

> [!info] Lichen (Latin: *lichen*, from Greek *leichen*) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A symbiotic association between an autotrophic algal partner (phycobiont) and a heterotrophic fungal partner (mycobiont), functioning as a composite organism.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Grows on tree bark, rocks, and soil in unpolluted environments; absent in polluted areas, making it a bio-indicator of air quality.

---

> [!info] Mycorrhiza (Greek: *mykes* = fungus + *rhiza* = root) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** A mutualistic symbiotic association between fungi and the roots of vascular plants, enhancing mineral absorption for the plant in exchange for carbohydrates.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Root systems of higher plants such as *Pinus*.

---

> [!info] Capsid (Latin: *capsa* = box/case) — [Exam Target: Both]
> **Scientific Definition:** The protective protein coat surrounding a virus's nucleic acid core, built from repeating protein subunits.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Outer covering of every virus particle, enclosing either RNA or DNA.

---

> [!info] Capsomeres (Latin: *capsa* = box + Greek *meros* = part) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** Individual protein subunits that assemble together to form the capsid of a virus.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Structural components of the viral capsid.

---

> [!info] Viroid (Latin: *virus* = poison + Greek *-oid* = resembling) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** An infectious agent smaller and simpler than a virus, consisting of a free, low-molecular-weight RNA molecule with no surrounding protein coat.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Discovered by T.O. Diener in potato tissue, causing potato spindle tuber disease.

---

> [!info] Contagium Vivum Fluidum (Latin: *contagium* = contact/contagion + *vivum* = living + *fluidum* = fluid) — [Exam Target: NEET]
> **Scientific Definition:** A term coined by M.W. Beijerinck describing an infectious agent (later identified as a virus) that could multiply only within living host cells, unlike bacteria.
> **Anatomical Location / Occurrence:** Historically identified in the infected sap of tobacco plants exhibiting mosaic disease.
